### Risks or ambiguities

- **Every figure is a relay figure.** The review's numbers come through WebFetch, and its own source file says the
  BSE rates and the BSDA tiers were not read raw (`sources/2026-10-28-broker-charge-pages.md:72`). Each card-bearing
  row begins with a raw read; until then no figure here is a card's `rate`.
- **D2's cost is duplication, permanently.** A BSE card repeats every rule of its NSE counterpart, so a later
  window edit must be made twice, and nothing in the build detects a pair that drifted. That is the price of a
  resolver that selects one card whole; the alternative (a merge or an override list) is a mechanism change and
  would need its own item.
- **D3's nil trap.** Because an absent fact makes a predicate false, an account with no `openedOn` or no holdings
  read is billed nothing unless a fallback rule charges it. The three shipped AMC goldens are the guard, and the
  row must keep them green without weakening an assertion.
- **D4's ambiguity is real, not accidental.** `charge_accounts` allows two accounts per person per broker
  (`ChargeAccountEntity.java:43`, unique on `{email, broker, demat account}`) and a trade names no account, so a
  lookup has no single right answer when there are two. The trade request stating its plan avoids the question;
  a lookup cannot.
- **D5 is a law-free decision with a law-sounding consequence.** The ₹20 fee is the broker's, so it is card data;
  but the new code must be classified in `RS-CG-CHARGE` — cost or transfer — exactly as `CLEARING` was (D-416),
  and that classification is the owner's ruling, not mine.
- **The stale source line is placed, not forgotten.** If D2 is deferred to (c), the "24 × NSE, 5 × BSE" correction
  still goes in that same row, which is then doc-only — or, if the owner prefers, as its own row.
