## Plan for W-237f — erased data in database snapshots, and a restore that must not bring it back

**For:** state, as configuration plus a runbook entry, when a person's erased data leaves the backups that MongoDB Atlas
keeps for WealthLens, and make sure a restore from an older backup cannot bring an erased person back (Act s.8(7)(b);
D-470 C2a).

### What I read (session 54)

- **Law, Gazette copies; sha256 matched** (`4deb2398…` Act, `eabc7d05…` Rules):
  - Act s.8(2), p.7: a Data Processor may be used *"only under a valid contract"*.
  - Act s.8(7), p.7: unless a law requires the data to be kept, (a) erase, and (b) *"cause its Data Processor to erase any
    personal data that was made available … for processing"* (source row 17).
  - Rules r.6(1)(d), p.26: *"reasonable measures for continued processing … such as by way of data-backups"*. Backups are
    one of the safeguards, so the answer cannot be to keep none.
  - Rules r.6(1)(f), p.26: the contract with the Processor must provide for security safeguards.
  - Rules r.8(3), p.27, Case 2: a cloud host *"also retains the data and associated logs for at least one year before
    erasure"*.
  - **No figure in the law limits how long a backup may be kept.** The period is the owner's choice. This plan uses no
    number of its own.
- **Repository:** nothing configures or describes backups. `grep -i atlas|backup|snapshot` finds only the runbook's
  "an Atlas snapshot keeps erased data until it expires (W-237f)", which appears three times (§1.z, §1.u, §1.t). The
  runbook's correction steps save `mongoexport` files to the owner's machine as their way back (§0.5, §1A, §2.1 – §2.5).
  Those files are WealthLens's own copies of personal data.
- **What a restore would undo:**
  - **Whole-account erasure:** an account is held by a `privacy_erasure_requests` row (`HeldAccounts.java:20`, an open
    row). The daily job erases only rows with `erased_at` unset (`AccountErasureService.java:53`).
    - A snapshot taken *before* a request loses that request.
    - A snapshot taken *after* an account was erased restores both the data and a row with `erased_at` set, so the data
      is never erased again.
  - **Single tax-profile erasure (W-235):** the request lives only on the profile itself (`erasure_requested_at`,
    `RetentionPurge.java:63`). After the purge erases the profile, nothing records that the erasure happened. A restore
    brings the profile back for good if the snapshot predates the request.
  - Logs, grievances and drafts that come back with a restore are erased again by age on the next run, so nothing is
    needed for them.

### Choices (recommended first)

**C1 — how long backups are kept.**
- **(a) Recommended: you set it in Atlas at no more than `app.privacy.log-retention` (P1Y).** The runbook states the
  result: erased data leaves every snapshot by `erased_at` + the longest snapshot retention.
  - Why the limit: an erasure record lives one year after `erased_at` (W-237e C4a). A snapshot older than that could hold
    a person whose record has already gone, and C2 could no longer find them.
  - A read-only check in the runbook compares the Atlas policy to the key.
- (b) A shorter limit of your choosing, such as 35 days. Erased data leaves the backups sooner, but a fault you notice
  late cannot be recovered from.

**C2 — a restore must not bring an erased person back.**
- **(a) Recommended: a record of every erasure, applied again on each run.**
  - Single-profile erasure leaves a record: a new `privacy_profile_erasures` row with `email`, `profile_id`,
    `requested_at` and `erased_at`. It is kept a year after `erased_at` by W-237e's `log-retention` job.
  - The daily `account-erasure` and `retention-purge` jobs erase again anything matching a record whose `erased_at` is
    set, and keep the first `erased_at`.
  - A runbook restore procedure: export both record collections from the live database before the restore and upsert
    them afterwards. The first run then erases again whatever came back, and the §1.u check confirms it.
  - Nothing to remember after a restore except the export and import.
- (b) The same records, but erased again only on a `SUPER_USER` call after a restore. This avoids daily delete calls
  (cheap at today's few accounts), but a restore logged without the call leaves erased people restored.
- (c) Runbook only, with no code. A single-profile erasure cannot be restored from anything, because nothing records it.
  Not enough.

**C3 — the owner's own `mongoexport` files.**
- **(a) Recommended:** a rule at the top of the runbook. Each such file is deleted once its step's result is logged in
  the run log, or once the longest snapshot retention from C1 has passed, whichever is first. The run-log line records
  the deletion.
- (b) No rule. These files are not a Processor's copy, but s.8(7)(a) reaches them all the same.

**C4 — the Processor contracts (s.8(2), r.6(1)(f)).**
- **(a) Recommended:** the runbook records which Processor terms you have accepted (Atlas, AWS): the document and the
  date. This is your action; I cannot read your accounts.
- (b) Place it as its own row, W-237h.

### Revision 1 (R13, session 54) — C2a is wrong; C2a′ replaces it

**Found before any code:** an erased email can register again. A request holds only while `erased_at` is unset
(`HeldAccounts.java:20`, `AuthService.java:47`). After the erasure, the login is gone and nothing refuses the email. C2a's
daily re-erasure deletes by email, so it would wipe a re-registered person's new account every night.

**What tells resurrected data apart from a new account is the snapshot's time.** A restore puts the whole database back
to the moment of snapshot S, and everything after S is lost, a re-registration included. So the data to erase again
belongs to exactly the erasures done *after* S: S's copy still holds that data, and nothing newer can be in the
database. An erasure done before S is already reflected in S, and erasing it again could only hit a re-registered account.

**C2 revised (recommended first):**
- **(a′) Recommended: erasure records, plus one `SUPER_USER` call after a restore that names the snapshot's time.**
  - `POST /privacy/erasure/reapply`, body `{"snapshotTakenAt": "yyyy-MM-dd'T'HH:mm:ss"}` (UTC).
  - It erases again every account whose request has `erased_at` after that time; `erased_at` is not moved.
  - It asks each `PersonalDataHolder` to erase again what it erased after that time. `taxplanning` erases each profile
    whose new `ProfileErasure` record (`email`, `profile_id`, `requested_at`, `erased_at`) is after it. Profile ids are
    never reused, so this is safe.
  - It answers with the accounts and profiles it erased again, and a second call changes nothing.
  - The time is required and refused if it is in the future.
  - Runbook restore procedure:
    1. Before the restore, export `privacy_erasure_requests` and the profile-erasure records from the live database.
    2. Restore.
    3. Upsert both exports.
    4. Make the call with the snapshot's time.
    5. Run §1.u's check: 0 left.
  - C1a's limit still holds. With snapshots kept no longer than P1Y, every erasure after S is under a year old, so its
    record still exists.
- (b′) Block an erased email from registering again for as long as its record is kept (a year after erasure). Then the
  daily re-erasure is safe and nothing is left to remember, but a person who comes back cannot do so for a year. That
  is a product change, and nothing in the law asks for it.
- (c′) Daily re-erasure limited to documents created before `erased_at`. This relies on a creation time in every
  collection, which I have not confirmed exists; older documents may lack one. Not recommended.

**Build changes from C2a:**
- No re-erasure inside the jobs.
- `AccountErasureService.eraseAgain(since)`, `PersonalDataHolder.eraseAgain(since)` (default: nothing), the call
  on the existing privacy controller, and `taxplanning`'s holder over the profile records.
