## Plan for W-079d — the taxable interest on the employee's own provident-fund contributions (`PF-EEINT-001`)

**What this item is for (one sentence):** bring into income from other sources, in both years, the interest a
provident fund credits in the year to the person's *taxable contribution account*. That account holds the person's
own contributions made since 1-4-2021 beyond ₹5,00,000 a year (a fund the employer does not contribute to) or
₹2,50,000 (any other), plus the interest on them. The trace also shows how much of this year's contribution goes into
that account.

**This plan raises choices (C1–C5); nothing is coded until you choose.**

### Findings

1. **The tax figure is the fund's, not a computation the law lets us redo.** r.277(1) / r.9D(1) make the taxable
   interest *"the interest accrued in the taxable contribution account"*. That account is the fund's book: every
   year's excess since 2021-22, plus interest, less withdrawals. The fund's rate and crediting method (EPFO's monthly
   running balance, for example) are prescribed by no provision I have read. Recomputing the interest from a rate
   would invent a method (R1). The fund reports the split; the EPF passbook shows it.
2. **This year's excess is still worth computing.** It is the part of this year's contribution r.277(3)(b)(i) puts
   into the taxable account, the figure T4-4 asserts (₹3,50,000). It is tested per fund: *"in that fund"* (1961),
   *"in such fund"* (2025). It is measured, not taxed: interest already accrued on it is part of the fund's figure.
3. **Both years have the same thresholds and the same rule.** Only the words differ: "previous year" and "tax year";
   Sl. 4's "financial year". Every regime: neither Act's regime provisions touch Schedule II Sl. 3–4 or s.10(11)–(12).
4. **An unrecognised provident fund is outside this rule.** Neither clause exempts its interest in the first place.
   The input's Javadoc will say it is not entered here.
