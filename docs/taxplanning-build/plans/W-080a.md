## Plan for W-080a — the leave travel concession's two-children limit

**What this item is for (one sentence):** stop the leave travel concession exempting the travel of more than two
surviving children, except children born before 1-10-1998 and multiple births after one child, in both years
(`SAL-LTA-CHILD-001`; D-159). Today a family with three or more later-born children is over-allowed (D-100).

**This plan raises choices (C1–C7) and one open question (OQ-27); nothing is coded until you choose.**

### Findings

1. **The limit is on children, not on journeys.** It can only bite where a journey's cost can be split by
   traveller. The WI's *"per-person fares"* is the input that's needed.
2. **It bites only where three or more restricted children survive.** A restricted child is one born on or after
   1-10-1998 and not part of a multiple birth after one child. Otherwise every child's travel is within the limit,
   and today's whole-journey figures stay right. So per-person figures need only be required when the limit can
   bite.
3. **The Rules don't say which two children are covered.** Sub-rule (6)'s *"multiple births after one child"*
   counts children in birth order, which points to the two eldest restricted children. That is an inference, so
   it is C3.
4. **r.278(1)(iii)(B)'s ₹30 per kilometre** prices *"the distance of the journey"*, not a person. The Rules don't
   say how much of it belongs to a child who isn't covered. → **OQ-27**.
5. **The 1998 date is law, so it belongs in the pack (R1).** Today it is spelled into an input flag's name. Reading
   it from the rule removes a second source of truth that could disagree with it.

### Choices (recommended first)

- **C1 — the rule:**
  - **(a) recommended:** a new row `SAL-LEAVE-TRAVEL-CHILDREN` with a definition `SurvivingChildrenLimit(children,
    exceptBornBefore)`:
    - `2` and `1998-10-01` in both years;
    - TY 2025-26 cites r.2B(4) (DoR compendium p.350), TY 2026-27 cites r.278(5)–(6) (Gazette 271092 p.1776);
    - `SOURCE_CHECKED`, `AllRegimes` as the journeys row. The new regime excludes the concession itself, through
      `DIS-NEW-TAX-REGIME-NO-EXEMPTION` (D-166), so the limit is never reached there.

    Relock with one version bump per year.
- **C2 — the input:**
  - **(a) recommended:** `Journey` gains `travellers`, a list of `JourneyTraveller(Optional<Integer> familyMember,
    Money amountIncurred, Optional<Money> fareAdmissible)`.
    - `familyMember` is an index into `FamilySnapshot.members`; empty means the employee.
    - An empty list means "not split", as today.
    - Where it is given, its amounts must add up to the journey's amount and fare, or the journey is refused.
  - **(b)** Only the uncovered children's share per journey. Smaller, but it puts the "which two" choice on the
    user.
- **C3 — which children are covered:**
  - **(a) recommended:** restricted children are `CHILD` members:
    - surviving on the journey's start date (no `dateOfDeath`, or a later one);
    - born on or after the rule's date;
    - not `multipleBirthAfterFirstChild`.

    The two eldest of them by date of birth are covered; the rest are not (finding 3, an inference recorded in the
    row's `_note`).
  - **(b)** The two whose travel costs most, the reading most favourable to the taxpayer. I don't recommend it: the
    text gives no basis for choosing by cost.
- **C4 — when the split is required:** where the family has more restricted surviving children than the limit,
  each journey must give `travellers`. A journey without them is refused, naming r.278(5) / r.2B(4). Otherwise
  journeys compute as today. A journey's exempt amount is then `min(Σ amount, Σ fare)` over its covered travellers.
  Each uncovered child's share gets its own nil line (`limitedBy` naming the limit), so nothing disappears from the
  trace.
- **C5 — `bornBeforeOct1998`:**
  - **(a) recommended:** read the date of birth against the rule's date, and remove the flag from `Member` (11 test
    sites; one `AssesseeTest` assertion goes with it). The Javadoc is corrected.
  - **(b)** Keep the flag, read it, and leave the date out of the pack. Not recommended (finding 5).
- **C6 — a per-kilometre journey with an uncovered child (OQ-27):**
  - **(a) recommended:** refuse it, naming OQ-27, until you rule. It is rare: no rail, no recognised public
    transport, and three or more later-born children.
  - **(b)** Apportion the per-kilometre fare by headcount. That is an inference about a figure.
