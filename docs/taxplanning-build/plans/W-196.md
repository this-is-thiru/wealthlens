## Plan for W-196 — the health-insurance cash and spreading refusals as nil lines

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** when a health payment is one the law does not let the
assessee deduct — paid in cash other than for a check-up (s.126(9)), or a lump sum claimed over several years on a
payment kind the spreading row does not reach (s.126(8)(b)) — the engine today stops the whole computation with an
exception, and this item asks whether it should instead accept the input and show a nil line for it, as s.133(5)'s
cash bar and the new regime already do.

**What goes wrong if the cash bar becomes a nil line and nothing else is decided.** Two questions the current throw
hides, because it never reaches them:

1. **Does the barred payment still count as a payment for s.126(7)?** `requireNotInsuredSenior` (`:246-265`) bars
   medical expenditure on a senior citizen for whom a premium was paid. It reads the raw payment list, filtered only by
   kind (`insurancePaid`, `:181-183`) — so a cash premium would still bar that senior's medical expenditure.
2. **Does it still raise its bucket to the senior row?** `coversSenior` (`:103`, set at `:146`) picks the cap row. A
   disallowed amount raising the cap would enlarge the deduction taken on the payments that remain.

### Choices (recommended first)

- **C1 — the cash bar (s.126(9)):**
  - **(a) recommended: a nil line, as s.133(5)'s donations.** The barred payment is recorded — `in` its amount, `out`
    zero, `limitedBy` naming `DED-HEALTH-CASH-PERMITTED` — and contributes nothing to its bucket: not its amount, not
    its senior flag. Nothing else in the run changes. This is the same instrument the code already uses for the bar it
    most resembles, and it keeps the total from including a claim the law does not allow (`12-claim-integrity.md:163`).
  - **(b) keep the refusal.** One unlawful input stops a whole computation that may have twenty lawful ones, and the
    user is told nothing about which payment or how much. It also contradicts the node's own treatment of the new
    regime, which gives a nil line for a payment the regime forbids.
- **C2 — the spreading bar (s.126(8)(b)):**
  - **(a) recommended: keep it a refusal — it is a different thing from a nil line — and place a row for the real
    question.** The spreading provision is about premiums; a scheme contribution's sum is deductible in the year it is
    paid (`DED-HEALTH-INSURANCE-LIMB`), and nothing the repo holds says a non-premium lump sum is disallowed. What the
    engine cannot decide is whether a multi-year claim on a scheme means "spread this" (which the law does not allow
    for it) or "I paid this much" (which the law does allow) — so refusing is still honest. The question that would
    end the refusal — *is the sum paid for a non-premium deductible in full in the year it is paid?* — is a law read
    that needs the words of s.126(8)(b), (10) and 1961 s.80D(4A), which the repo does not hold (`W-156b2` risk 2); it
    becomes a row, not a guess in this item.
  - **(b) a nil line of the whole payment.** The literal words of the item's row, but it disallows a sum the law allows
    (the scheme limb) on the strength of a spreading rule that never applied to it. I would not do this.
  - **(c) claim the sum in full in the year paid (`1/1`), the line noting the years were not applied.** The likely law
    answer, and the most generous — but it needs the sub-sections' text before it can be asserted, and it turns the
    user's 10,000 into 20,000 without a source. That is C2a's placed row, not this item.
- **C3 (only if C1a) — a cash-barred premium and s.126(7):**
  - **(a) recommended: still bar that senior's medical expenditure.** The law's condition is whether "no amount has
    been paid to effect or to keep in force the health insurance" — the amount was paid, though not deductible. The
    read is the one the code has always had; C1a changes only what the total may include.
  - **(b) a payment the engine disallows counts for nothing.** Then a cash premium neither deducts nor bars the
    medical expenditure, and the user keeps one of the two. Cheaper for the user, and not what the text says.

### The five roles

- **Architect:** the refusal and the nil line are different instruments — one says "this input cannot be evaluated",
  the other says "this claim is not allowed". Cash is the second; spreading is still the first, so the item splits.
  The nil line is the same one s.133(5) already uses, so nothing new is invented.
- **Product manager:** a user who paid a premium in cash would rather be told "nil, citing the rule" than lose the
  whole computation. The deduction figure changes only for an input that today produces no figure at all.
- **Senior engineer:** one method's second caller, one early return, one line, three tests. The barred payment is
  excluded from the bucket and from the senior flag, so no cap moves; s.126(7) is untouched (C3a).
- **Automation tester:** the two refusals' tests are rewritten to assert the line and prove themselves by the two bite
  probes — restore the throw (the nil-line tests fail) and drop the return (the barred 10,000 deducts, so case 4 fails).
- **Manual tester:** after the build, run the four health payments above through the harness and read the trace: the
  nil line names the row, carries the amount, and the total excludes it.

  cash bar is tested first. Both are true; the pin records the choice rather than leaving it to a reader.
