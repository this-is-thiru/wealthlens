**What is left of the item once the stale half is removed:** a `Condition` record, a `ConditionContext` component and
accessor, a JSON tag with its parse arm, two exhaustive-switch arms, and the tests that exercise them — a closed

rules turn on it" a false statement.

### Choices (recommended first)

- **C1 — `PaidOtherThanInCash`:**
  - **(a) recommended: remove it end to end.** Delete the record; drop its `permit` and its two switch arms in
    `Condition`; drop `ConditionContext`'s `paidOtherThanInCash` component, accessor, null-check term, both
    constructor arguments and the Javadoc paragraph that explains it; drop the tag and parse arm in `ConditionJson`;
    drop the argument at the four 10-argument construction sites and the `claim` helper's `nonCash` parameter; update
    the counts (twelve predicates plus three item facts), the two `@DisplayName`s and the tag-list test. Nothing
    becomes unreachable that is used today: the item-kind rows added by W-196 ask which kinds may be paid in cash,
    and s.133(5)'s cash test lives in `DonationDeduction` against a data threshold.
  - **(b) wire it to the cash bar.** Replace `DED-HEALTH-CASH-PERMITTED`'s `health.kind = PREVENTIVE_CHECKUP` with a
    payment-mode test. It would take a new fact path (the mode is on `HealthPayment`, not in `ItemFacts`), a change to
    a row W-196 has just built and tested, and the law's own shape is "these kinds may be paid in cash" — which the
    item-kind condition states directly. It would also invert the row's meaning: a row named *cash permitted* would
    hold for a non-cash payment of any kind.
  - **(c) keep it, unwired.** The Javadoc would have to say it is unused, which is a comment standing in for a
    caller, and CLAUDE.md §2 forbids the parameter without one.
- **C2 (only if C1a) — the two counts that ride on it:** the `Condition` Javadoc's "all thirteen predicates … four
  structure" becomes twelve and three; `02-TECH-SPEC.md:60`'s list is **corrected as well** — drop `PaidOtherThanInCash`
  and add the `ItemDateBefore` it has been missing since W-088f2b (D-291) — so the number stays fifteen and the list
  becomes true. I read that as part of the same edit rather than a drive-by: leaving a list that names a predicate that
  no longer exists, in the same sentence I am editing, would be the worse option.
