## Plan for W-114c — the calendar's health-insurance and own-NPS deduction days

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** extend W-114a's "last day to pay for a deduction" entry
to the s.126 health limbs (80D) and the s.124(3) own-NPS deduction (80CCD(1B)). Each entry is priced by the tax the
unused room would save.

**Design sections I read:** W-114c's row (D-666); `plans/W-114a.md` and D-666; `20-product-gaps.md` F30;
`00-SUMMARY.md:360` (`D-MARGINAL`).

**What the pack and code have (read this session):**
- **The four health limbs are each `AggregateWithCeiling`, and each is chosen per payment by its facts.**
  - `DED-HEALTH-FAMILY` and `-FAMILY-SENIOR` cover self, spouse and children; `-PARENTS` and `-PARENTS-SENIOR`
    cover parents. Each set is `EXACTLY_ONE`, and a payment reaches its row by `health.covered.relationship` and
    `AgeAtLeast 60` of the insured.
  - Each row's ceiling is ₹50,000, and **every contributor has a sub-cap.** A premium is capped at ₹25,000
    (₹50,000 for a senior) and medical expenditure at ₹50,000. So W-114a's carrier, the first contributor without a
    sub-cap, does not exist here.
- **A `HealthPayment` names whom it covers:** `coversAssessee`, and `familyMembers` by position. `FamilySnapshot`
  members carry `Relationship` (`SPOUSE`, `CHILD`, `PARENT`, `SIBLING`, `DEPENDANT_OTHER`) and a date of birth.
- **Own NPS.** `DED-NPS-OWN` (s.124(3)) has `EMPLOYEE_NPS` and `NPS_MINOR_DEPOSIT` as contributors, a ceiling of
  ₹50,000 and no sub-cap. `EMPLOYEE_NPS` is a `SALARIES` pay line of kind `EMPLOYEE_CONTRIBUTION`, and it counts
  first under Schedule XV up to its 10 %-of-salary sub-cap. Only what that sub-cap leaves reaches s.124(3)
  (`ScheduleXvDeduction.java`, "returns what the pension sub-cap left"). `NPS_MINOR_DEPOSIT` is for a minor's
  account only (s.124(4)).

**Choices for the owner:**
- **C1: scope.**
  - **C1a (recommended):** the health limbs and own NPS, the NPS part gated by the probe below. If an
    `EMPLOYEE_NPS` pay line changes the salary income, the NPS part goes to a placed row instead (R13).
  - C1b: the health limbs only, with own NPS placed as W-114d.
- **C2: how the room is found.**
  - **C2a (recommended): fill by probe.** For each party, one more run, with a payment as large as the largest
    ceiling among the rows it could reach (read from the pack):
    - **Assessee:** an `INSURANCE_PREMIUM` covering the assessee, not in cash, for one year.
    - **Parents:** an `INSURANCE_PREMIUM` covering every `PARENT` member, where the family lists one (C4).
    - **Own NPS:** an `EMPLOYEE_NPS` pay line on the first employment, of `DED-NPS-OWN`'s ceiling plus Schedule XV's
      (so the 80C share and the s.124(3) room are both filled), where the draft has an employment.

    The **room** is the rise in the deduction lines of the rows the payment reached, read off the trace, and that is
    also the amount to pay, since within a cap each rupee paid is a rupee deducted. The **saving** is the fall in
    the bill. The engine chooses the row, limb and senior rate inside the run, so nothing is chosen outside it
    (D-252).
  - C2b: compute each limb's room in Java from its caps and the insured's age. That chooses rows outside the run
    and puts the senior threshold in code (CLAUDE.md §4), so I don't recommend it.
- **C3: the entry.** `DEDUCTION_PAID_BY` on the year's last day, as in W-114a:
  - `rule` = the row(s) whose line rose (e.g. `DED-HEALTH-PARENTS-SENIOR`);
  - `amountToPay` = the room;
  - `paidByThen` = what those rows counted before;
  - `costOfMissing` = the saving.
  - **C3a (recommended):** shown only where the saving is above nil, which is none in the default regime. The own-NPS
    entry's saving includes any 80C share it fills, which overlaps W-114a's Schedule XV entry. The response already
    says entries overlap.
  - C3b: shown always.
- **C4: parents.**
  - **C4a (recommended):** one premium covering every listed parent, since the parents' limb is one ceiling for
    them together.
  - C4b: one entry per parent.

## Re-plan (R13, 2026-11-14) — own NPS's amount to pay is not its room

**What broke:** C2a priced every party as "the rise in the deduction is both the room and the amount to pay". For
own NPS that is false once Schedule XV is full. A probe on the old regime (basic 10,00,000, 1,50,000 of tuition fees,
so Schedule XV is at its ceiling) found:

| Own NPS paid as a pay line | `DED-SCHEDULE-XV` (in → out) | `DED-NPS-OWN` | Bill |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | 1,50,000 → 1,50,000 | — | 75,400 |
| 50,000 | 2,00,000 → 1,50,000 (aggregate ceiling) | none | 75,400 |
| 1,50,000 | 2,50,000 → 1,50,000 | 50,000 | 65,000 |

The engine counts own NPS under Schedule XV first, up to its 10 %-of-salary sub-cap, and s.124(3) takes only what
that sub-cap leaves (`ScheduleXvDeduction`, "returns what the pension sub-cap left"). So when Schedule XV is full,
₹1,00,000 of the ₹1,50,000 earns nothing. The amount to pay is 1,50,000, not the 50,000 of room. Whether that ordering
is the law (2025 s.124(3) against Schedule XV para 1(y); 1961 s.80CCD(1B), "whether or not any deduction is allowed
under (1)" as commonly read, which I have **not** checked in a Gazette copy) is a question this item cannot settle.

The health part is unaffected: a premium's room is exactly what the premium buys (probe: ₹50,000 covering the
assessee raised `DED-HEALTH-FAMILY` by its ₹25,000 sub-cap and the bill fell by 5,200).

**Rulings needed:**
- **R1a (recommended):** build the health limbs now and move own NPS to a placed **W-114d**. W-114d first reads
  2025 s.124(3) and Schedule XV para 1(y) (Gazette 265620), the 1961 s.80CCD(1), (1B) chain and the amending Acts, and
  settles whether the engine's sub-cap-first order is right. That is a possible engine defect, and the entry's amount
  to pay depends on it.
- R1b: build own NPS now with no `amountToPay` (the room and saving only), and the note "the contribution needed
  depends on Schedule XV's room".
- R1c: build own NPS now and find the amount to pay by search (several more runs per calendar).
