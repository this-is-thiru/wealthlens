## Plan for W-156f2 — gift measure by limb

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** s.92(2)(m)(i) – (iii) (1961 s.56(2)(x)(a) – (c)) give
each kind of receipt its own threshold and its own measure, but `GiftIncome:69–81` maps a kind to its limb with a
`switch`, and one `GiftThreshold` on `OS-GIFT` stands for every limb. This item makes each limb a row, reached by
what was received and whether anything was paid for it, and carrying its own threshold and measure (D-351, W-156f
C2a).

**This plan raises C1 – C4. No amount changes. Gift trace lines change their rule and text (C4).**

- **The limbs, from `OS-GIFT`'s note** (2025 Act s.92(2)(m), p.139; the 1961 Act the same, `sources/2026-10-17-gifts.md`
  A1, B1):
  - (i) "any sum of money without consideration, the total of which exceeds ₹ 50000, the whole of such sum";
  - (ii) immovable property, (A) without consideration: the stamp duty value above ₹ 50000; (B) for a consideration:
    the excess "more than the higher of … (I) ₹ 50000; or (II) 10% of the consideration";
  - (iii) other property, (A) without consideration: "the aggregate fair market value of which exceeds ₹ 50000";
    (B) for a consideration "less than the aggregate fair market value … by an amount exceeding ₹ 50000", the
    excess.
- **The measure is one formula.** For a gift, or a year's total, the charge is value − consideration, where that is
  more than the tolerance, the higher of the threshold and the row's share of the consideration (nil where the row
  has no share). A year's total carries no consideration, because (i) and (iii)(A) are "without consideration". This
  gives (ii)(A) and (ii)(B) as `immovablePropertyCharged` does today, (iii)(B) as `propertyShortfallCharged`, and
  (i) / (iii)(A) as `isExceededBy`. So what differs by limb is data: the threshold, the share, and whether receipts
  are totalled for the year or tested one by one.
- `relock_rulepacks.py:27` refuses a removed rule.
