**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** for each claim in a draft that evidence bears on, show the
amount the law allows on the facts, the amount the person's documents support today, and what would close the
difference (F23) — in rupees the engine computed, never a judgement of risk.

**Probe (2026-11-15; `DR 12` C2, C7 and the inputs read, no code run):**
- **The design's own example is a second computation** (`12-claim-integrity.md:94-112`, on T3-1's facts): rent
  receipted for 8 of 12 months at ₹30,000 → evidenced rent ₹2,40,000; the salary and the period stay whole; limb (b)
  becomes 2,40,000 − 1,32,000 = ₹1,08,000 against ₹2,28,000 allowed; gap ₹1,20,000. That is the engine run on the
  same facts with the tenancy's rent cut to its receipted months (W-119 C5a).
- **The cuts W-119b's record allows:** a tenancy's `rentPaid` (`taxengine/input/Tenancy.java:18`), a journey's
  expenditure (`JourneyTraveller.amountIncurred`, `JourneyTraveller.java:17`), a qualifying payment's `amount`
  (`QualifyingPayment.java:14`). `EnteredFacts` already has `withTenancies` (`EnteredFacts.java:318`) and
  `withQualifyingPayments` (`:294`); it has **no** `withJourneys`.
- **Which rules are "claims evidence bears on"** is not listed anywhere, and listing them in code would be law in
  code. A run with **nothing** evidenced finds them: every rule whose lines differ from the run as it stands.
- **Landlord's particulars:** the engine already asks for them on the first HRA line when rent exceeds the threshold
  (`ExemptionNodeTest.java:253`); a draft names the profile's landlords (`DraftInputs.ThirdParties`), and
  `Landlord` holds name, address and PAN (`taxplanning/profile/Landlord.java:33-41`). Which landlord is which
  tenancy's is not recorded (W-119b's risk), so "on file" is said per draft.
- **Language** (`12-claim-integrity.md` C7): "Fully evidenced" / "₹X not yet evidenced"; never "safe", "low risk"
  or a peer figure (T12-14, W-119d's copy test).

**Choices for the owner:**
- **C1: how the claims and their gaps are found.**
  - **C1a (recommended):** three runs of the same draft — as it stands; with every evidenced figure cut to what is
    held; with nothing held. A rule whose lines differ between the first and third is a claim evidence bears on; its
    allowed amount is the first run's, its evidenced amount the second's, the gap their difference. No list of rules
    in code; a rule added to the pack later is covered with no change.
  - C1b: two runs and a hand-kept map from each evidence kind to rule ids — law in code, and wrong the day a rule is
    added.
- **C2: where the cut-down facts are built.**
  - **C2a (recommended):** in `taxplanning`, from the draft's entries and `EvidenceHeld`, using `EnteredFacts`' own
    `with…` methods; `EnteredFacts` gains `withJourneys` beside them. The engine is asked ordinary questions.
  - C2b: a pricing method inside the engine that takes the evidence — the engine would then know what evidence is.
- **C3: rent for receipted months.**
  - **C3a (recommended):** the tenancy's rent in proportion to its receipted months (8 of 12 → two-thirds), the
    period and salary unchanged, as the design's example does. Said in the response: rent is taken as equal each
    month.
  - C3b: ask the person for each month's rent — a new input, for a difference that matters only when rent changed
    within one tenancy.
- **C4: what the panel says closes a gap.**
  - **C4a (recommended):** per kind, one content sentence filled from the facts — the months still unreceipted, the
    expenditure or payment not yet evidenced — plus, where the engine asked for the landlord's particulars, whether
    the draft's landlords have them on file. Drafted for your adoption at the build's review; no "safe".
  - C4b: amounts only, no words.
- **C5: the address.** `GET /tax/user/{email}/drafts/{draftId}/claim-integrity` (recommended), or another path.

**Risks or ambiguities I noticed:**
- Three runs per read; the build measures one read on the benchmark persona on the pipeline and reports it against
  N1 before close-out.
- Cutting one claim's facts moves other rules' lines (less rent can raise total income and so a percentage limit
  elsewhere). Those rows appear with a gap that is real but indirect; the response's `notIncluded` says a gap can be
  the effect of another claim's missing evidence.
- A line that **rises** when evidence is cut (a deduction that takes over, as rent paid under s.134 might) gives a
  negative gap; the build shows such a row with both amounts and no "gap to close" wording — decide the words at
  review.
