### Findings

1. **No children's choice exists.** The limit is one figure per child, and the model reads it against the allowance
   received in total (D-101 C3). Which two children are counted cannot change the exemption, because none is
   distinguishable. A choice would exist only if per-child amounts were entered and the limit read child by child,
   which D-101 did not adopt.
2. **No gratuity allocation exists either.** The model holds at most one other-gratuity payment in a year, and the
   earlier years' allowance (B) is fixed input. With one payment against A − B, there is nothing to allocate.
3. **But the one-payment model is wrong when two employers pay gratuity in the same year.** Their amounts are summed
   into one payment, measured on the first employer's salary and its single separation, and capped once. That is
   neither refused nor right: each gratuity has its own least-of (own ten-month average, own completed years), and
   only the A − B ceiling is shared. The same pattern applies to `OTHER_LEAVE_ENCASHMENT` (`LEAVE_ENCASHMENT`, the
   same `paid` / `employmentOf`). This is a silent wrong number (CLAUDE.md standard 2, *"Nothing silent"*). It is
   also exactly where `DR 15`'s gratuity choice would arise: how a shared A − B is split between two gratuities.
