## Plan for W-326b — "what would change it", the words

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** a line whose least-of limit binds on a lever gains A4.4's
third sentence, "paying ₹X more in rent would raise it to ₹Y", worded by the content and shown by the renderer.

**Probe (the rule data, each lever's node site, the renderer, `Money`; 2026-11-14):**
- **Where a C2 lever drives a limb** (14 rules):
  - `RENT_PAID`: HRA, rent paid.
  - `HRA_RECEIVED`: HRA.
  - `ALLOWANCE_RECEIVED`: children's education, conveyance, hostel and uniform allowances.
  - `AMOUNT_INCURRED`: conveyance, uniform, leave travel.
  - `AMOUNT_CLAIMED`: disabled dependant (×2), specified disease (×2).
  - `TREATMENT_AND_STAY`: medical treatment abroad.
  - `INTEREST`: savings and senior deposit interest.
- **Finding 1 — four of the seven are not advice.** Where the received amount itself binds (`HRA_RECEIVED`,
  `ALLOWANCE_RECEIVED`, `INTEREST`), receiving more raises the exemption or deduction by exactly what is added to
  income, so the tax does not change. That is A4.4's own point: "increasing HRA alone would not". `TREATMENT_AND_STAY`
  is the employer's spend on treatment abroad. Its line (`MedicalTreatmentAbroadNode:95`) shows the perquisite after
  the exclusion, not the capped figure, and does not carry headroom. Only spending levers lower tax: `RENT_PAID`,
  `AMOUNT_INCURRED` and `AMOUNT_CLAIMED`.
- **Finding 2 — where headroom reaches the line.** It travels only through `withCapResult` (W-326a).
  - `ExemptionNode` (HRA), `AllowanceExemption`, `LeaveTravelExemption` and `DisabilityDeduction` use
    `withCapResult`, so their lines carry it.
  - `RentPaidDeduction:77` writes the cap's text with a suffix through `withLimitedBy`, so its line drops it.
    `DepositInterestDeduction:111` does the same (an `INTEREST` lever).
- **The renderer** builds what, then why (`ExplanationRenderer.explain`); a third sentence goes after the why.
- **Rounding:** `Money.roundToMultiple` truncates paise first, so it cannot round up. The engine's figure is exact
  (W-326a).

**Choices for the owner:**
- **C1: the first levers (revisits D-695 C2 with finding 1).**
  - **C1a (recommended):** the spending levers only: `RENT_PAID`, `AMOUNT_INCURRED`, `AMOUNT_CLAIMED`. The other four
    stay silent, since they are content and can be added later with their own wording.
  - C1b: all seven, as approved, with a sentence that is true but saves no tax for four of them.
- **C2: the sentence.**
  - **C2a (recommended):** one sentence per lever in content (`headroom`, keyed by input, with `{more}` and `{next}`),
    because the verb differs (paying rent, spending, incurring). The compiler refuses a key no shipped `LeastOf` limb
    is raised by, and any other placeholder.
  - C2b: one shared sentence with a word per input ("{more} more {input} would raise it to {next}"). It reads stiffly.
- **C3: the figure.**
  - **C3a (recommended):** `{more}` rounded up to the whole rupee, since paying that much does reach the limit. This
    needs a small `Money.upToWholeRupee()`. `{next}` is shown as it is.
  - C3b: exact, to the paisa.

**Draft wording (Claude's, for your adoption; no digits):**
- `RENT_PAID`: "Paying {more} more in rent would raise it to {next}."
- `AMOUNT_INCURRED`: "Spending {more} more on what this allowance is for would raise it to {next}."
- `AMOUNT_CLAIMED`: "Spending {more} more on this would raise it to {next}."
