## Plan for W-079e2 — the TY 2025-26 interest allowance shared by dividends and units (OQ-26 Q2)

**What this item is for (one sentence):** where the 1961 s.57 proviso's 20% limit binds over interest claimed
against both dividends and Mutual Fund units, attribute the allowed interest between them in proportion to each's
income (D-229 C2). That fixes the dividend income the surcharge's 25%/37% test takes out, and lifts D-200 C3a's
refusal.

**This plan raises choices C1–C3. Nothing is coded until you choose.**

### Findings

1. **A pure proportional share can attribute interest an item never bore.** Take dividends of 10,00,000 bearing
   1,00,000 and units of 10,00,000 bearing 5,00,000. The allowed interest is 4,00,000, so the dividends' half is
   2,00,000. That is more than the 1,00,000 claimed against them. The head's arithmetic never deducts more than an
   item's own expense, so a proportional share needs bounds to be consistent with it.
2. **The bounded share collapses the existing special cases into one rule.** Bound the dividends' share below by
   (allowed − units' claim) and above by the dividends' claim. Then:
   - nothing claimed on dividends gives nil;
   - all the claim on dividends gives everything allowed;
   - nothing limited gives each its own claim.

   Those are the three paths `interestAllowedOnDividends` takes today, now one formula.

### Choices (recommended first)

- **C1 — the attribution:**
  - **(a) recommended:** the dividends' share = allowed × dividends received ÷ base, bounded to
    [allowed − claimed on units, claimed on dividends] (finding 1). Since allowed ≤ claimed, the bounds never cross.
    This is your proportional rule wherever it gives each side no more than it claimed.
  - **(b)** the pure proportional share, unbounded. It is the ruling's words read literally. In finding 1's case it
    takes out 2,00,000 of interest against dividends that bore 1,00,000.

  - The class Javadoc's OQ-26 sentence about the shared allowance is updated. The W-088 refusal (l.168–182, the 15%
