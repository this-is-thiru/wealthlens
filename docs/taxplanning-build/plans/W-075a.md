### Findings

1. **Schedule XV payments are not income under any head.** A life-insurance premium or tuition fee is a sum paid,
   not a receipt; the component model requires a head. `EMPLOYEE_PF` and `EMPLOYEE_NPS` sit naturally under
   SALARIES (withheld from pay, like professional tax); the other 24 do not.
2. **Five clauses carry a limit that one sub-cap per contributor cannot state:**
   - **(a)** life insurance — para 2's percentage of each policy's sum assured, by issue date and life covered;
   - **(c)** a Government deferred annuity — "to the extent of 20 % of salary", and Schedule XV does not say which
     salary;
   - **(j), (k), (l)** — ULIPs and the annuity plan: whether para 2 ("an insurance policy, other than a contract
     for a deferred annuity") reaches them is not settled by the words I read.
3. **(y) has two limbs:** (i) 10% of salary for an employee — the existing `SAL-SALARY-FOR-EMPLOYEE-PENSION` base;
   (ii) 20% of gross total income for anyone else — no GTI figure exists yet.
4. **s.124(1) depends on the employer and s.124(2) on the regime:** 14% for a Government employer in every regime;
   10% for any other employer in the old regime; 14% for any other employer in the new regime.
5. **The other conditions are entry facts, not amounts** — (b) no cash option; (q) any two children, no
   development fee; (r) para 3; (w) three-year lock-in. They are `W-076`'s (or the entry form's) to enforce; each
   component's note names its condition.
