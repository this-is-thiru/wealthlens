- **What the Gazette says about the employer, so far:**
  - **ss.123 – 126 (pp.159–162):** a word search finds "employer" only in s.124(1), the employer's own NPS
    contribution (W-117c's). Each section is still to be read in full at build.
  - **s.19(1) Table Sl. 1 (p.41):** "Sum paid by the assessee as a tax on employment" — entire amount; no employer
    condition.
  - **s.22 (pp.47–48):** no employer condition. The higher limit needs "a certificate from the person to whom
    interest is payable" (s.22(2)(a)(ii), (4)) — the lender, so that certificate is evidence the law itself asks for.
    A house-property loss is one of the five particulars s.392(4)(a) lets a person give the employer "at his option",
    so here that sentence is a true basis.
  - **Sch. III Table Sl. 8 (p.505):** the sum must be "received by, or due to, such individual from his employer".
    So leave travel differs from the rest: with no travel assistance from the employer there is nothing to claim;
    where assistance was paid but taxed, the return takes the claim. r.278's conditions are still to be read.
- **The model meets two things it does not hold yet:**
  - **One entry, several rules.** Health has ten rules, home-loan interest five, leave travel four. A catalogue has
    one entry for a rule, and W-117d's coverage check (I23) will count rules.
  - **Figures that are caps.** A placeholder resolves only a rule's `definition` field. The investment limit and the
    health limits are caps, not definition fields, so those entries cannot name their limit by placeholder. Home-loan
    interest can (`HP-OWN-RESIDENCE-INTEREST-CAP.aggregate`, `.anyOtherCase`).
- **1961 basis (TY 2025-26).** The rules' 1961 provisions (ss.80C, 80CCD(1B), 80D, 24(b), 10(5), 16(iii), 10(13A))
  were read on the thc.nic.in consolidated Act (current to Act 7 of 2019), which their provenance already rests on —
  not a Gazette copy.

**Draft content (Claude's, for your adoption; no digits; every legal statement to be read in the Gazette first):**
- **Investments:** start from the provident fund already taken from your salary; add what you paid in the year
  (premiums, tuition fees, home-loan principal, the listed savings); give the proofs to your employer by the cut-off;
  claim the rest in your return. *Evidence:* a receipt or statement for each payment, dated in the tax year.
  *Mistakes:* forgetting the provident fund already counts; counting home-loan interest here; counting fees that are
  not tuition.
- **Own NPS:** check how much of your contribution is already counted with your investments; pay the rest into your
  own account by the tax year's end; give the statement to your employer or claim it in your return. *Mistake:*
  counting the same rupees in both places.
- **Health:** pay premiums other than in cash; get the insurer's certificate with the period covered; note your
  parents' ages; spread a premium paid for several years. *Mistakes:* paying a premium in cash; claiming medical
  expenses and a premium for the same person.
- **Home-loan interest:** get the lender's certificate for the year, showing any later loan taken to repay the first;
  tell your employer of the loss if you want less tax deducted; claim it in your return either way. *Mistakes:*
  counting principal as interest; missing the completion condition for the higher limit.
- **Leave travel:** travel in India and keep the tickets; claim through your employer's process; check the journeys
  already used in the current block of years. *Mistakes:* claiming without travelling; treating the block as tax
  years.
- **Tax on employment:** check your payslips for the tax your employer took and paid to the State; where you paid
  it yourself, keep the receipt and claim it in your return. *Mistake:* claiming tax that was due but not paid in
  the year.
