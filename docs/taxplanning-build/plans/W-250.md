## Plan for W-250 — the notice before consent

**For:** a person registering is shown what WealthLens holds about them and why, and how to withdraw, use their rights
and complain, *before* consenting, and the app can later prove both. Today `POST /auth/register` gives no notice and
records no consent (`auth/service/AuthService.java:45-62`).

### Choices (recommended first)

**C1 — the basis for processing. A legal reading; yours.**
- **(a) Recommended: consent, with the notice before it.** This is the row's premise. s.6(10) puts the proof on the app, so the
  record below is what the app could show in a proceeding.
- (b) s.7(a), voluntarily provided data: no s.5 notice and no consent record. W-250 would close as a decision, with no code.
  Not recommended: its illustrations are one-off services (a pharmacy receipt, a broker search), not an account kept for
  years. If the reading fails, every account would have no basis at all.

**C2 — how registration proves the notice came first (if C1a).**
- **(a) Recommended: the notice carries a version, and registration must echo it.**
  - The version is the sha256 of the notice as published, computed at startup. Wording changes are configuration, so the
    version follows them with no number kept by hand.
  - `GET /privacy/rights` and the `/privacy` page show the version.
  - `POST /auth/register` requires `privacyNoticeVersion`. A missing or old version is refused with 400: *"read the
    privacy notice at GET /privacy and register with its version"*.
  - On success, `privacy_consents` records `{email, notice_version, given_at}`. The caller cannot echo a version without
    first fetching the notice, so the version is the evidence that the notice came first.
- (b) A boolean `consentGiven`. Not recommended: any caller sets it, and it does not say which notice was shown (W-247 C2b's
  reasoning).

**C3 — withdrawing consent (s.6(4)). The row asks for this decision.**
- **(a) Recommended: asking for account erasure (W-237d, `POST /privacy/erasure/user/{email}`) is the withdrawal.**
  - Giving consent takes one request and withdrawing takes one, so the two are equally easy.
  - The hold stops all processing at once (s.6(6)), and r.8(3)'s year then runs before erasure (s.8(7)'s "any law").
  - The notice says so. The request row proves the withdrawal, so no new endpoint or record is needed.
- (b) A separate withdrawal endpoint. Not recommended: it would hold and erase exactly as erasure does.

**C4 — what the notice gains (r.3(b), (c)).** All of it is configuration under `app.privacy`, and startup refuses a start
without any of it. The page and the JSON show it.
- `personal-data`: an itemised list, one entry per holder above, saying what it holds (for example *"Tax planning: profiles,
  salary details, computations and drafts, and who read them"*).
- `purpose`: the services the data is processed for (portfolio tracking, P&L and charges, insurance records, tax
  planning).
- `withdrawal`: the C3a sentence and its endpoint.
- `board-complaint` (C5).

**C5 — complaining to the Board. I cannot source a link.**
- **(a) Recommended: the sourced description now, and the link when you give it.**
  - Now: *"If your grievance here is not resolved (raise it first: Act s.13(3)), complain to the Data Protection Board of
    India, which works as a digital office (DPDP Rules r.20)."*
  - Later: adding the link is a yaml edit with no code. W-237c already asked you for it.
- (b) Hold W-250 until you give the link.

**C6 — accounts registered before this (s.5(2)).**
- **(a) Recommended: tell them at sign-in.**
  - Every `POST /auth/login` response gains `privacy_notice` (the `/privacy` link) and `privacy_notice_version`.
  - An account with no `privacy_consents` row gets one at its first sign-in: `{email, notice_version, noticed_at}`, with no
    `given_at`. That row proves the notice was given.
  - Processing goes on, as s.5(2)(b) allows. No runbook command is needed.
- (b) Refuse sign-in until the person consents through a new endpoint. Not recommended: s.5(2)(b) does not require it, and
  it locks people out.

**C7 — found on the way, unplaced: registration lets the caller choose their own role.**
`AuthService.java:59` sets roles from the request. `AuthHelper.Role` includes `SUPER_USER` (`auth/dto/AuthHelper.java:17`),
so anyone can register as a super user.
- **(a) Recommended: place it as row W-255.** It does not belong to W-250's scope.
- (b) Fold it into W-250. Not recommended: it is a different reason to change.

### Build (if C1a – C7a)
1. **`shared/privacy`:**
   - `PrivacySettings` gains `personalData`, `purpose`, `withdrawal` and `boardComplaint`, each refused if absent.
   - `PrivacyNotice` carries these and `version`.
   - `PrivacyConsent` is the entity (`privacy_consents`). `PrivacyConsents` records consent at registration and the
     notice at the first sign-in; it is idempotent through the `email` key.
   - `PrivacyRecordErasure` also erases the person's consents.
2. **`auth`:**
   - `RegistrationRequest.privacyNoticeVersion`. `AuthService.addUser` refuses a missing or old version, then records the
     consent.
   - The `LoginResponse` gains its two fields, and `generateToken` records the notice.
3. **Configuration and page:** the four yamls and the test yaml get the C4 / C5 text. `templates/privacy/notice.html`
   shows the new sections and the version.
4. **Callers:** the three integration tests register with the version, and `cleanDatabase()` adds `privacy_consents`.
   In `api-collection`, Register User gains the field, and Login and *Read the Privacy Notice* gain notes. The W-217
   check runs before the push.
5. **Docs:** row W-255 (C7a); the runbook entry (below); STATE and CHECKPOINT.

### Tests (by hand)
- **Registration:**
  - Register with the version that `GET /privacy/rights` returns → 201, and one `privacy_consents` row with that version
    and `given_at`.
  - Without the field → 400. With `"0"` → 400. Neither leaves a `user_details` row.
- **Version:**
  - A `PrivacySettings` with one word changed in `purpose` has a different version. The same settings built twice have
    the same version.
- **Existing accounts:**
  - An account saved without a consent signs in → the response carries the link and the version, and one `noticed_at`
    row exists.
  - A second sign-in adds no row.
  - A newly registered account's sign-in adds no row.
- **Notice content:**
  - The page and the JSON name each `personal-data` entry, the purpose, the withdrawal endpoint, s.13(3) and r.20.
- **Erasure and startup:**
  - Account erasure removes the consents.
  - Startup refuses each of the four new settings when it is absent.
- **Probes** (each restored by `cmp`):
  - Skip the version check → the 400 test fails.
  - Drop the email filter on the notice record → the second-sign-in test fails.
  - Drop the consents from erasure → the erasure test fails.

### Out of scope
- Translation into Eighth Schedule languages: s.5(3) and s.6(3) read "English **or** any language …", and the notice is
  in English.
- Asking for consent again when the notice changes; the version is recorded so a later item can.
- Per-purpose consent (portfolio and tax planning asked separately).
- Any client screen (W-251).

### Review lenses
- **Architect:** auth uses `shared/privacy` as it already does, with no new module edge. The version and times are facts,
  and the wording is configuration (CLAUDE.md §4).
- **PM:** registration needs one more field. Existing users see the notice link at sign-in. A client shows it (W-251).
- **Senior engineer:** a refused registration writes nothing. The notice record is written once per account. One read of
  `privacy_consents` per sign-in.
- **Automation tester:** the cases and probes above, on the unit and integration tiers.
- **Manual tester (staging):** read `GET /privacy` and copy its version; register without it and with it; sign in an
  older account and read its `privacy_consents` row.

### Post-deployment (R15)
No command. Read-only check: before deploy, the count of `privacy_consents` is 0. After deploy, its rows with `noticed_at`
grow as older accounts sign in. A second run is safe. The way back: redeploy the earlier build; the collection can stay or
be dropped, and no other data changes. Any client must now send `privacyNoticeVersion` to register. WL-A-2 republished
at close-out.

### Risks
1. **C1 is a legal reading. Mine is not authoritative.**
2. The itemised list can drift from the holders. A test ties each `PersonalDataHolder` bean to a `personal-data` entry
   by a key, so adding a holder without an entry fails the build.
3. An older account that never signs in gets no notice. Its purpose lapses (W-249) and it is erased, so no processing
   continues without the notice.

**Size:** medium — about 3 new classes, 6 changed, 5 yamls, 1 template, 3 tests changed and 1 new integration class.

### Build record

- **Where the build differs from the plan text, and why:**
  - The four r.3 settings are one nested record, `app.privacy.consent` → `ConsentNotice`, not four flat keys. Flat keys would
    give `PrivacySettings` 12 components (CLAUDE.md §4: more than 7 means a missing type).
  - The item type is `PersonalDataItem`, not `HeldPersonalData`: `ArchitectureTest` bans the `Data` suffix.
  - Registration answers 200, as it did before, not 201.
  - `cleanDatabase()` needed no change: it already empties every collection.
  - `PrivacyConsents` is not `@Transactional`. Each write is one document, and a duplicate key inside a Mongo transaction
    would abort it rather than be caught as a record already made.
- **Main code:**
  - `shared/privacy`: `ConsentNotice`, `PersonalDataItem`, `PrivacyConsent` (`privacy_consents`, unique `email` through
    `PrivacyIndexInitializer`), `PrivacyConsentRepository` and `PrivacyConsents` are new. `PrivacySettings` gains
    `consent` and computes the version. `PrivacyNotice` gains `consent`, `version` and `PAGE`. `PersonalDataHolder` gains
    `noticePart()`, and each of the five holders implements it. `PrivacyRecordErasure` erases the consents.
  - `auth`: `RegistrationRequest.privacyNoticeVersion`. `AuthService.addUser` checks the version before writing anything,
    then records the consent. `generateToken` records the notice. `LoginResponse` gains `privacy_notice` and
    `privacy_notice_version`, both camelCase with `@JsonProperty`, so nothing new goes under the file's MemberName
    suppression.
  - The four yamls and the page.
- **Tests:**
  - `ConsentNoticeIntegrationTest` (8 tests). `PrivacySettingsTest` gains 9.
  - `AuthIntegrationTest` and `PurposeLapseIntegrationTest` register with the version. The two failing-store doubles name
    a part.
- **Probes** (run together, restored by `cmp`): each failed the tests it targets.
  - No version check → the two 400 tests failed.
  - The notice rewritten at every sign-in → both sign-in tests failed.
  - Consents not erased → the erasure test failed.
  - The `insurance` entry removed from the test yaml → the holder test failed.
- **api-collection:** a new *Privacy / Read the Privacy Notice as JSON* entry, whose script stores
  `PRIVACY_NOTICE_VERSION`. *Register User* sends it and documents it. *Login* and *Request Erasure of the Account* gain
  notes. Every entry parses (Ruby YAML), and the Register body maps strictly onto `RegistrationRequest`.
