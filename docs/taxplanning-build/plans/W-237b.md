## Plan for W-237b — grievances recorded and answered within the published period

**For:** a user can raise a grievance about how their personal data is handled, gets a receipt saying by when it will
be answered, and reads the answer; the owner has a queue that shows what is due, so none passes the period
(Act s.8(10), s.13; Rules r.14(3), r.9; D-470).

### What I read (session 51; Gazette copies re-fetched, sha256 `4deb2398…` and `eabc7d05…` match the sources file)

- **Act s.8(10)** (248045 p.8): *"A Data Fiduciary shall establish an effective mechanism to redress the grievances of
  Data Principals."*
- **Act s.13(1)**: *"readily available means of grievance redressal … in respect of any act or omission of such Data
  Fiduciary … regarding the performance of its obligations in relation to the personal data of such Data Principal or
  the exercise of her rights"*. **(2)**: *"respond to any grievances … within such period as may be prescribed from the
  date of its receipt"*. **(3)**: she *"shall exhaust the opportunity of redressing her grievance under this section
  before approaching the Board"*.
- **Rules r.14(3)** (267650 p.30), **verbatim, as printed**: *"Every Data Fiduciary and Consent Manager shall prominently
  publish on its website or app, or both, as the case may be, within a reasonable period not exceeding ninety days under
  its grievance redressal system for responding to the grievances of Data Principals and shall, for ensuring the
  effectiveness of the system in responding within such period, implement appropriate technical and organisational
  measures."* The printed text has no object for "publish". I read it as: **publish the period** (reasonable, at most
  ninety days) within which the system responds, and put measures in place so responses arrive within it. Your call (C2).
- **Rules r.9:** the contact is named in every response to a request exercising a right; W-237a's guard test
  (`PrivacyNoticeIntegrationTest.RESPONSES`) holds each listed request to it.
- **What exists:** `GET /privacy/rights` lists s.13 with *"Write to the contact…"* and no endpoint
  (`application.yaml:36-38`). Nothing records a grievance; no mail dependency in `backend/pom.xml`, so the app cannot
  write to a user. `UserValidator` matches `/user/{email}` to the token and lets SUPER_USER through
  (`auth/service/UserValidator.java`). "Grievance" is a new word in the code (grep: yamls only).

### Choices (recommended first)

**C1 — where it lives.** (a) **`shared/privacy/` beside W-237a's notice** — a `privacy_grievances` collection,
`Grievance` entity, `GrievanceService`, `GrievanceController`. One place for the app's privacy duties; no new module
dependency. (b) A new `privacy` module — cleaner boundary, but W-237a's notice and contact would have to move too, which
is outside this item.

**C2 — the period (r.14(3)).** (a) **The response period is configuration, `app.privacy.grievance.response-period`
(proposed `P30D` — your figure), published in the notice; beside it `longest-period-allowed: P90D` with its source in a
yaml comment, and a start refused if the first exceeds the second.** Both are data, so the ninety days is not a Java
constant. Each receipt carries its `respond_by` date, fixed when the grievance is received (a fact: what was promised).
(b) Publish ninety days itself and promise no shorter period.

**C3 — the measures to answer in time.** (a) **A queue for the owner and a recurring runbook check:**
SUPER_USER `GET /privacy/grievances` lists unanswered grievances by `respond_by`, oldest first; SUPER_USER
`POST /privacy/grievances/{id}/answer` records the answer and when it was given. A runbook recurring operation:
read the queue weekly. No "overdue" flag is stored or served: the facts are `received_at`, `respond_by` and
`answered_at`. (b) As (a) plus a daily job logging a warning for each grievance within N days of `respond_by`. A log
line nobody reads is not a measure, so I would not add it without an alert that reaches you.

**C4 — the grievance text at rest.** The text is personal data. The tax cipher (`taxplanning/protection/FieldCipher`)
is inside a CLOSED module and `shared` may not depend on it. (a) **Store the text as the other non-tax modules store
theirs (plain), and place a row: encryption of personal data outside `taxplanning` (r.6(1)(a)), app-wide.** (b) Move the
cipher into `shared` now. That touches W-134's code and both tax keys, which is a separate item's work.

### Build (if C1a – C4a)
1. `shared/privacy/`: `Grievance` (`@Document("privacy_grievances")`: `email`, `text`, `received_at`, `respond_by`,
   `answer`, `answered_at`; UTC via `TLocalDateTime.atUtc()` and the injected `Clock`), `GrievanceRepository`,
   `GrievanceService`, records `GrievanceRequest`, `GrievanceReceipt` (id, received, respond_by, answer, answered_at,
   **contact**), `GrievanceAnswer`. `PrivacySettings` gains `grievance` (response period, longest allowed, both
   required) and the notice publishes the period.
2. `shared/controller/GrievanceController`: user `POST` and `GET /privacy/grievances/user/{email}` (their own, with
   answers); SUPER_USER `GET /privacy/grievances` and `POST /privacy/grievances/{id}/answer`. A blank text, or an answer
   to an answered grievance, is a 400.
3. The four yamls: s.13's means points at `POST /privacy/grievances/user/{email}`; the grievance period keys.
4. Backlog: W-237d and W-237e name `privacy_grievances` (erasure and the one-year retention cover it); the C4a row placed.

### Out of scope
Telling the user by email (no mail in the app; the client shows the receipt and the answer, W-247); erasing or
retaining grievances (W-237d, W-237e); encryption (C4a's row); the website or app (W-247).

### Post-deployment (R15)
Yes, no data change: runbook recurring operation: weekly, the owner reads `GET /privacy/grievances` and answers
anything nearing its `respond_by`. Read-only, safe to repeat, nothing to roll back. The new collection is created on the
first write; no index precheck (lookups are by email on a small collection). WL-A-2 republished after the commit.
