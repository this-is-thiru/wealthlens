### Choices (recommended first)

**C1 — is a property's expense cost or transfer expenditure? (the owner's ruling; rows 1–5)**

- **(a) Recommended: by side, as D-375 C2a.** Paid on the purchase → cost of acquisition (s.72(1)(b) / s.48(ii)); paid on
  the sale → transfer expenditure (s.72(1)(a) / s.48(i)). Being a cost of acquisition, the buy-side amount is **indexed
  with the price** in the land cap (row 7), which this item fixes. The rows' `_note` says the ruling is the owner's and
  that the Acts do not say.
- (b) Stop and source further first: W-088g9 goes `BLOCKED` until a CBDT or Gazette text is found or a prompt is
  answered. Honest, but the search above found none, and a judgment would still need your ruling.

**C2 — which expenses have a code.**

- **(a) Recommended: `STAMP_DUTY`, `BROKERAGE` (existing), and new `REGISTRATION`, `LEGAL_FEE`.** The row names three;
  I add legal fees because row 3's list names them and a conveyancing fee would otherwise have nowhere to go. The new
  codes join the two `RS-CG-CHARGE` rows only, not `charge-catalogue.json`, which is what rate cards may charge: no
  broker bills them, and there is no seed or runbook change.
- (b) The row's three only (`REGISTRATION` new). A legal fee is then refused.

**C3 — the shape of the entry.**

  transfer together and writes one `trade_outcomes` row. No new collection, no holding lifecycle, no erasure change:

### Tests (figures by hand)

**Case A, long-term.** Bought 1-6-2015 for 40,00,000, with stamp duty 2,80,000, registration 40,000 and brokerage 40,000
(expenses 3,60,000). Sold 1-9-2025 for 1,00,00,000, with brokerage 1,00,000 and legal fee 20,000 (1,20,000).

```
Gain            1,00,00,000 − 1,20,000 − 40,00,000 − 3,60,000       = 55,20,000     (LONG_TERM_OTHER)
At 12.5 %       55,20,000 × 12.5 %                                  = 6,90,000.00
Indexed cost    43,60,000 × 376 ÷ 254                               = 64,54,173.23
Indexed gain    1,00,00,000 − 1,20,000 − 64,54,173.23               = 34,25,826.77
At 20 %         34,25,826.77 × 20 %                                 = 6,85,165.35
Tax charged     the lesser (s.197(3))                               = 6,85,165.35
```

**Today's code would index the price alone:** 59,21,259.84 → indexed gain 35,98,740.16 → 20 % = 7,19,748.03, so the
lesser is 6,90,000.00: **₹4,834.65 too much tax.** That is what makes row 7's test able to fail. The exact paise are
asserted as the Money type rounds them, checked at build against this working.

**Case B, short-term.** Bought 10-1-2025 for 20,00,000 with stamp duty 1,00,000; sold 1-9-2025 for 25,00,000 with
brokerage 50,000. Gain 25,00,000 − 50,000 − 21,00,000 = **3,50,000**, in `SHORT_TERM_SLAB`.

**Rule data, both years, both sides:** `REGISTRATION` and `LEGAL_FEE` deducted on the right limb, and every
`PropertyExpense` covered by a row in every shipped year (the `ChargeCatalogueCoverageTest` pattern), so a code added to
the enum without a row fails.

### Risks or ambiguities

1. **C1 is a ruling, not a finding.** The Acts are silent and the only texts found are not holdings. The rows will say so,
   and a figure resting on them is only as firm as the ruling.
2. **Row 7 changes a law computation** beyond the row's stated scope. It belongs here because this item is what makes it
   reachable: leaving it would ship a known overcharge. Only land reaches that path.
3. **The index for a buy-side expense runs from the year of acquisition,** with the price. That assumes it was paid in that
   year; an expense paid in a later year has no date in this entry.
4. **Sizing:** under §7 with C3a; over it with C3b, hence the split offered there.
