### Findings

1. **DR 19's inference is wrong, and the row repeats it.** A missing marginal relief makes tax jump **up**
   at the threshold. Without `RATE-SUR-MR-001`, ₹10 over 50L costs ₹1,12,323 (`DR 08` T2-2), and "more
   income never means less tax" still holds. The relation a missing relief violates is **bounded marginal
   cost**: extra income ΔI never raises payable by more than ΔI × 1.04. The two reliefs cap tax (plus
   surcharge) at the extra income (`RATE-SUR-MR-001` W = U + V; `REBATE-NEW-TAX-REGIME-EXCESS`), and the
   cess adds "four per cent. of such income-tax and surcharge" (FA 2026 s.3(15)). Monotonicity is still
   worth having: it's what an **over**-generous or misplaced relief violates.
2. **Bounded marginal cost has one lawful exception.** In the First Schedule the s.87A / s.156(1) rebate
   has no marginal relief (H-3; `DR 08` T1-O2 is its test). ₹10 above ₹5,00,000 costs ₹13,000, so the
   property must exempt exactly that step, sourced, and nothing else.
3. **Three of the five properties have no input yet**, because the graph is entered at total income:
   - deductions arrive with `W-076` (`ChapterEightNode`);
   - salary components with `W-060`/`W-061`;
   - proration with `W-066b` (`TemporallyApportionedCap`).

   Writing them now would test stubs.
4. **`traceSumsToTheTotal` can be stated without a new API:** `tax.netpayable` = Σ `out` of the TAX lines −
   Σ `out` of the RELIEF lines. That's the slab tax, the unrelieved surcharge and the cess, less the
   rebates and the surcharge relief; ROUNDING lines are excluded. It's what P5 promises: the trace
   explains the figure.
5. **Random sampling wouldn't do what DR 19 hopes.** A relief's binding zone is narrow (~₹1.6L at 50L,
   `DR 08` T2-3) inside ₹0–₹6Cr, so jqwik's default 1,000 random tries seldom land in it. A **deterministic
   sweep** hits every threshold by construction, runs in known time, and fails the same way every time.
6. **Standing rules (D-043):** none apply — there are no rules, sections or concept IDs. Both years run.

### Choices (recommended first)

- **C1 — tooling.**
  - **(a) recommended:** no new dependency. Plain JUnit sweeps over a fixed grid, with each property
    checked on every adjacent pair.
  - **(b)** Add jqwik. That's a dependency, a version to verify, and random sampling that would miss the
    zones (finding 5).
- **C2 — the properties now:**
  - **(i) monotone:** payable never falls as income rises;
  - **(ii) bounded marginal cost:** ≤ 1.04 × ΔI, except the First Schedule step across ₹5,00,000;
  - **(iii) the trace reconciles:** finding 4.

  The other three go to `W-076`, `W-060`/`W-061` and `W-066b` as Appendix F lines, each with the item it
  must land in.
- **C3 — the grid.** Step ₹10,000 from ₹0 to ₹1,00,00,000, and ₹50,000 from there to ₹6,00,00,000. That's
  ~2,000 incomes per cell. Every statutory threshold (5L, 12L, 50L, 1Cr, 2Cr, 5Cr) is on the grid, and each
  missing relief breaks the bound at its first step by a wide margin: at 50L, ~₹1.1L against a bound of
  ₹10,400. The cells:
  - the new regime, both years;
  - the First Schedule, both years, at 40;
  - ages 65 and 82 (their own ladders) over ₹0–₹1Cr only, where the ladders differ.

  That's ~12,000 runs, about 4.3 s at the measured cost. Properties use `tax.netpayable`, unrounded, so
  the ₹10 rounding adds no noise.
- **C4 — errata.** `W-058` row and `DR 19` E4.1 (an addition-only erratum block): the property that catches
  H-1 is bounded marginal cost, not monotonicity. Finding 1, with the T2-2 figures.
