## Plan for W-160d — the P&L by facts (split proposal)

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** each year's P&L document splits realised amounts by
`portfolio`'s own term — a short/long pair and a by-classification map — decided by two one-year tests; this item
replaces that with a split by asset type and segment, facts the tax side can read, and stops `portfolio` deciding a
term there (D-362; T1 P6).

**This item is bigger than §7 and changes a response and stored data. This plan builds nothing: it asks C1 – C4 and
proposes a split (C3). On approval I place the rows, record D-366, write the first sub-plan, then stop.**

### C1 — the shape of the split

- **(a) recommended:** `gains_by_asset_and_segment`, a map of `AssetType` to a map of `TradeSegment` to
  `FinancialReport`, in both `realised_profits` and `out_sourced_realised_profits`. Enum-keyed maps, as
  `gains_by_classification` is today. It is summable: it holds facts, not heads of income. A sale with no recorded
  segment goes under `DELIVERY`, the entry default (D-295), as `ProfitLossContext.segment()` already does.
- **(b)** a list of `{assetType, segment, report}` entries. Easier to extend with another fact later, but awkward to
  update in place and to read.

### C2 — the stored P&L documents

The pair and the map on existing documents cannot be split by asset or segment: the facts were summed away.

- **(a) recommended:** a one-time `SUPER_USER` operation that rebuilds each document's split from `trade_outcomes`,
  which keeps the asset type and segment of every sale, and then unsets the pair and the map. Its sub-plan settles
  which figures it can rebuild (first bullet under "Not read yet"), and stops if a year cannot be.
- **(b)** unset the pair and the map only. Years before the change show charge summaries and nothing realised, until
  something rebuilds them.
- **(c)** leave the old fields on old documents, unread. Stored term labels stay, which §4 bars.
