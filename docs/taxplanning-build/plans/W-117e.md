## Plan for W-117e — donations priced as a claim

> **Approved as recommended — C1a, C2a, C3a; draft content adopted** (owner, session 100, 2026-11-15). Every
> path and figure below was read on 2026-11-15 at `84793e5e`; s.133 was read in the Gazette copy (2025/265620
> pp.167 – 169).

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** give the rules that actually deduct a donation an entry, so
a person's donations show up among the claims made, with a measured worth, instead of being listed unpriced. The
reported-donation entry keeps saying when a donation needs the institution's report.

**Probe (2026-11-15; code run as a throwaway test, then deleted):**
- **Why donations are unpriced today:** the one donation entry is led by `DED-DONATION-UNREPORTED`, the s.133(6) report
  bar (W-118, owner's option (a)). A bar never writes a deduction line, so `ClaimPricing` never finds the claim.
  `DED-DONATION-WHOLE`, `-HALF` and `-QUALIFYING` sit on `notYetCatalogued` (W-117d).
- **Withholding the three deducting rules prices the claim:**
  - Persona: basic pay 10,00,000, old regime, with ₹20,000 to a named fund and ₹10,000 to a reported charitable
    institution.
  - `WHOLE` writes 20,000 and `HALF` 5,000, both as `DEDUCTION` lines.
  - With `{WHOLE, HALF, QUALIFYING}` withheld, both lines go to nil and the bill rises from 1,01,400 to 1,06,600.
  - So the worth is 5,200, which is 20.8 % of 25,000 and agrees with the hand figure.
- **s.133, read in the Gazette (pp.167 – 169):**
  - **(1)(a):** the whole sum, to the named funds.
  - **(1)(b):** half, to the drought fund, a registered charitable institution or fund ((b)(ii)), government, housing
    authorities, minority corporations and places of worship.
  - **(2):** the qualifying limit, over (a)(xxiii), (xxiv) and (b)(ii) – (vi).
  - **(4):** a sum of money only.
  - **(5):** no deduction for a donation over the limit paid in cash.
  - **(6):** the donee's report, for (b)(ii) only.
  - **No employer condition anywhere.** r.205(2) Sl. 4 covers Chapter VIII (W-117c C21).
- The cash limit is a definition field (`DED-DONATION-CASH.atMost`, both versions), so a step can name it by
  placeholder. The qualifying limit is a cap, so it is not named (W-117b C2a).

**Choices for the owner:**
- **C1: the entry's shape.**
  - **C1a (recommended):** one new entry, "donations", led by `DED-DONATION-WHOLE` covering `-HALF` and
    `-QUALIFYING`. It is priced as one claim, since the qualifying limit caps both rates together. The reported-donation
    entry stays as it is, unpriced, as the guidance for (b)(ii)'s report. The three rules leave `notYetCatalogued`.
  - C1b: two entries, the whole rate and the half rate. The qualifying limit would have to sit in one of them while it
    caps both, so the worths would be wrong.
- **C2: the new entry's channel.**
  - **C2a (recommended):** `EMPLOYER_OPTIONAL`, `CLAIMABLE_IN_RETURN`, Part B, as W-117b's and W-117c's Chapter VIII
    entries: no employer condition, and r.205(2) Sl. 4.
  - C2b: `RETURN_ONLY`.
- **C3: the cash bar.**
  - **C3a (recommended):** `DED-DONATION-CASH` stays on `notAClaim` as applying without a claim (W-117d). The new
    entry's step names its limit by placeholder.
  - C3b: the new entry covers it too. Withholding a bar would run the donation as if the bar did not exist, which is
    the wrong measure.

**Draft content (Claude's, for your adoption; no digits; each legal statement from s.133 as read):**
- **appliesTo:** donations of money to a fund, institution or body that section 133 names. For a charitable
  institution or fund that must report your donation, see also the reported-donation entry.
- **Steps:**
  1. Give by any way other than cash if a donation is more than {{DED-DONATION-CASH.atMost}}.
  2. Get a receipt from the fund or institution, with its name and the amount.
  3. Give the receipt to your employer on their declaration form if you want less tax deducted.
  4. Otherwise claim it in your return.
- **Evidence:** the receipt for each donation in the tax year.
- **Mistakes:**
  - Giving goods or services instead of money.
  - Paying more than the limit in cash.
  - Counting the same sum again under another section.
