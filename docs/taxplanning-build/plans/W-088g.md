## Plan for W-088g — land or building acquired before 23-7-2024

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** a resident individual or HUF who sells land or a building
bought before 23-7-2024 pays no more long-term capital-gains tax than 20 % of the gain measured with the cost indexed
up to the year of sale — and today nothing in the pack, the input or the portfolio can express it: no asset kind for
land or building exists, the index table starts at 2023-24, and the engine's per-part rate rows each read one base at a
rate in a ladder, so a "least of two taxes on two measures" is not among the shapes a rate row may take.

**The engine.** `CapitalGainsInput.Bucket` carries `consideration`, `cost`, `charges` and `BucketFacts`
(`input/CapitalGainsInput.java:72-74`); `BucketFacts.assetKind` is a **free string** (`:139`) passed straight through, so the
engine can already be *told* "LAND" — nothing validates the value at the input, the refusal comes later and loudly.
`CapitalGainsAdapter.inputFor` is the only path that builds a tax-engine `CapitalGainsInput`
(`taxplanning/portfolio/CapitalGainsAdapter.java:49-56`), and it sends `bucket.assetType().name()` (`:75`).

**The parts.** `CG-PARTS` declares four parts in both years (`law/rules/CG-PARTS.json`, both versions), and the five
`CG-PART-*.json` rows carry `capitalgains.asset = EQUITY` in every version (measured: `LONG-LISTED`, `LONG-NOTIFIED`,
`LONG-OTHER`, `SHORT-LISTED`, `SHORT-SLAB`). A bucket no row covers is refused, not silently charged:
`CapitalGainsNode:158` — "fall in no row of `RS-CG-PART` … so no provision is known to charge them".

**The portfolio.** `AssetType` has six constants — EQUITY, MUTUAL_FUND, BOND, GOLD_BOND, FD, INSURANCE
(`portfolio/dto/enums/AssetType.java:4-9`); **no land or building**. `CapitalGainsSummaryService` groups into buckets by
a key that includes the asset type and does not filter on EQUITY (`portfolio/service/CapitalGainsSummaryService.java:115-131`),
so a new kind would flow through unchanged.

**The cap vocabulary.** A rate row's `cap` is a `CapShape`; `LeastOf` — "the least of several named limbs", each limb
naming its shape — is one of them and is declared in pack JSON (`rules/cap/LeastOf.java`, `rules/json/ShapeJson.java:103,115,140`).
`PercentOfBase` takes a rate and a **base identifier** (`rules/cap/PercentOfBase.java:20`), the base set living in the
pack rather than a Java enum (`rules/id/BaseId.java:12-18`), and `CapContext` holds a **map** of bases
(`rules/cap/CapContext.java:39-43`). So "the least of 12.5 % of the gain and 20 % of the indexed gain" is expressible
as data — *if* the node hands in both bases.

**What blocks it in code.** `TaxByBasisNode` builds the context for a special-rate part from **one** base —
`CapContext.of(((SlabLadder) rateVersion.cap().orElseThrow()).base(), part.getValue())` (`TaxByBasisNode.java:101`) —
and it *casts the cap to `SlabLadder`*, so a `LeastOf` cap would throw. The slab path does the same for the total
(`:85`, `:91`), and `shiftShortfall`/`nilBandCeiling` read `ladder.base()` and the ladder's nil band. Nothing on this
side holds an *indexed* gain: `CapitalGainsHead` carries `Map<CapitalGainsPart, Money>` of gains and the losses left
(`pipeline/CapitalGainsHead.java:19`) and no second measure.

**The index table.** `RATE-COST-INFLATION-INDEX` (`law/rules/RATE-COST-INFLATION-INDEX.json`) holds **2023-24 = 348,
2024-25 = 363, 2025-26 = 376** for TY 2025-26 and adds **2026-27 = 384** for TY 2026-27. Its `_note` says in terms:
"capital gains are not built" and "a year the table does not hold is refused". Its other reader, `PerquisiteNode`,
turns a missing year into a refusal, not a default (`PerquisiteNode.java:180-184`, D-115, OQ-11 as built).

**The pack says so itself.** `RATE-CG-LONG-OTHER`'s `_note` ends: *"Not modelled: s.197(3) / the s.112(1)(a) second
proviso (land or building acquired before 23-7-2024 — no input carries the asset kind; the adapter maps equity only) and
s.197(4) / s.112(1)(c)(iii)"*.

### Findings

1. **The arithmetic is the same in both years; the enacting provision is not.** Every transfer in TY 2025-26 or
   TY 2026-27 is "on or after 23-7-2024", so 1961 s.112(1)(a)(ii)(B) and 2025 s.197(1)(b) are both in play and the
   comparison is the same in both: `min(12.5 % × gain, 20 % × indexed gain)`. One row, two versions, one shape.
2. **The measure is not the land gain alone, and that is an open legal question (OQ-36).** A is "income-tax computed
   under sub-section (1)(b)" — s.197(1)'s *whole* long-term base, not the land slice; B is the same computation at
   20 % with indexed cost. Whether the comparison runs over all of s.197(1)'s gains or only the land gain changes the
   answer whenever there is another long-term gain in the same year, and the pack cannot be written both ways.
3. **The interaction with the shortfall shift and set-off is a second open question (OQ-37).** A and B are both
   "computed under (1)(b)", which is the gain after the s.197(2) shift to the basic-exemption band and after
   s.108(2) set-off; re-measuring B's gain with indexed cost changes the amount shifted, so the shift may have to be
   recomputed inside B rather than reused.
4. **Nothing can reach it today, and reaching it is two items.** `AssetType` has no land or building; the adapter sends
   the enum's name; all five part rows require EQUITY. Adding the kind to the portfolio **alone** would make every land
   sale throw at `CapitalGainsNode:158` — a live breakage, not a gap. So the kind and the part row that reaches it are
   one change.
5. **The index table cannot serve it.** 2001-02 through 2022-23 — **22 years** — are absent, and acquisitions on or
   before 22-7-2024 are exactly the ones the provision covers, so only an acquisition between 1-4-2023 and 22-7-2024
   could be indexed against the three years held. Sourcing the 22 is its own item with its own gazette reads.
6. **Extending the shared table is not neutral.** `RATE-COST-INFLATION-INDEX` is also what `PerquisiteNode` reads, and
   that node *refuses* a year the table lacks (D-115, OQ-11 as built). Adding 22 years would silently widen what an
   accommodation valuation accepts; a capital-gains-only rule is one way out, and revising D-115 is another. That is a
   decision for the item that sources the years, not a detail of this one.
7. **Two code sites are unavoidable whatever is decided.** `TaxByBasisNode:101` casts a cap to `SlabLadder` and supplies
   one base; and the head has no indexed gain to supply as the second base. The first is a shape change, the second is a
   new measure carried from `CapitalGainsNode`.
8. **The WI row's premise is too strong in one place.** It says the indexed gain must be "sent" on `CapitalGainsInput`.
   It need not be: the engine holds the acquisition date (a `BucketFact`), the sale date, the cost and the index — it can
   compute the indexed cost itself. What must arrive from `portfolio` is only the **asset kind**.

### Risks or ambiguities

1. **The split names work that does not exist yet.** W-088g1 and W-088g2 are real items with real sourcing and real code;
   placing them is not a promise that the cap is close. If the owner would rather wait for the index years before
   spending anything, the honest alternative is to leave W-088g unbuilt and unsplit — but the two questions then have no
   home, and the next reader starts where this session started.
2. **OQ-36 is not a stylistic question.** Under one reading the comparison runs over the whole of s.197(1)'s gains, so a
   land sale's tax depends on unrelated long-term gains in the same year; under the other it does not. The plan states the
   literal reading; if the owner reads it the other way, W-088g's shape changes materially.
3. **The index table may have to be split in two.** If W-088g1 decides a capital-gains-only rule, the pack gains a second
   `IndexByYear` rule and `PerquisiteNode` keeps its own; if it extends the shared one, D-115 and OQ-11 come back for a
   ruling. Either way W-088g1's plan carries the decision, not this one.
4. **W-088g2 will look like a portfolio feature and is really a tax one.** A land sale that is recorded and then refused
   by the engine is worse than one that cannot be recorded; the part row must land in the same change, and its golden
   must show a charged land bucket, not merely a stored one.
5. **Nothing here is proven by a failing test**, because nothing is built. The proof this session can offer is that the
   documents are consistent and the backend is untouched — reported as what it is, not as a green build of new code.
