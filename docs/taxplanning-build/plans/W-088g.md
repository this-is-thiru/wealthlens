### Findings

1. **The arithmetic is the same in both years; the enacting provision is not.** Every transfer in TY 2025-26 or
   TY 2026-27 is "on or after 23-7-2024", so 1961 s.112(1)(a)(ii)(B) and 2025 s.197(1)(b) are both in play and the
   comparison is the same in both: `min(12.5 % × gain, 20 % × indexed gain)`. One row, two versions, one shape.
2. **The measure is not the land gain alone, and that is an open legal question (OQ-36).** A is "income-tax computed
   under sub-section (1)(b)" — s.197(1)'s *whole* long-term base, not the land slice; B is the same computation at
   20 % with indexed cost. Whether the comparison runs over all of s.197(1)'s gains or only the land gain changes the
   answer whenever there is another long-term gain in the same year, and the pack cannot be written both ways.
3. **The interaction with the shortfall shift and set-off is a second open question (OQ-37).** A and B are both
   "computed under (1)(b)", which is the gain after the s.197(2) shift to the basic-exemption band and after
   s.108(2) set-off; re-measuring B's gain with indexed cost changes the amount shifted, so the shift may have to be
   recomputed inside B rather than reused.
4. **Nothing can reach it today, and reaching it is two items.** `AssetType` has no land or building; the adapter sends
   the enum's name; all five part rows require EQUITY. Adding the kind to the portfolio **alone** would make every land
   sale throw at `CapitalGainsNode:158` — a live breakage, not a gap. So the kind and the part row that reaches it are
   one change.
5. **The index table cannot serve it.** 2001-02 through 2022-23 — **22 years** — are absent, and acquisitions on or
   before 22-7-2024 are exactly the ones the provision covers, so only an acquisition between 1-4-2023 and 22-7-2024
   could be indexed against the three years held. Sourcing the 22 is its own item with its own gazette reads.
6. **Extending the shared table is not neutral.** `RATE-COST-INFLATION-INDEX` is also what `PerquisiteNode` reads, and
   that node *refuses* a year the table lacks (D-115, OQ-11 as built). Adding 22 years would silently widen what an
   accommodation valuation accepts; a capital-gains-only rule is one way out, and revising D-115 is another. That is a
   decision for the item that sources the years, not a detail of this one.
7. **Two code sites are unavoidable whatever is decided.** `TaxByBasisNode:101` casts a cap to `SlabLadder` and supplies
   one base; and the head has no indexed gain to supply as the second base. The first is a shape change, the second is a
   new measure carried from `CapitalGainsNode`.
8. **The WI row's premise is too strong in one place.** It says the indexed gain must be "sent" on `CapitalGainsInput`.
   It need not be: the engine holds the acquisition date (a `BucketFact`), the sale date, the cost and the index — it can
   compute the indexed cost itself. What must arrive from `portfolio` is only the **asset kind**.

   perquisite cap already uses. Improvements are the only element of s.48's indexed cost the input cannot express.
3. **The qualifying list, the date and the 1-4-2001 floor are law, so they are rule data** (CLAUDE.md §4): a one-row set
   on `REGIME_AND_CONDITIONS` with `IsResident`, `HasPersonType([INDIVIDUAL, HUF])`, `ItemFactIs(capitalgains.asset,
   LAND_OR_BUILDING)` and `ItemDateBefore(capitalgains.acquired.on, 2024-07-23)`; its definition names the floor year. A
   bucket no row matches simply has no indexed measure — no Java `if` anywhere.
4. **No row split is needed for residence.** Because the qualifier is refused by facts, a non-resident's land has no
   indexed measure, so B = 20 % of the gain > A = 12.5 % and the cap cannot bind. `RS-RATE-CG-SPECIAL` keeps its five
   rows and the 12.5 %-only assessees keep their arithmetic without a second rule id.
5. **The measure must survive both set-off stages, by construction.** Within the head, `CapitalGainsNode` absorbs its own
   losses into `available` (`:119-120`); across heads, `SetOffNode`'s absorption and `CarriedLossSetOff` lower the same map
   (`SetOffNode.java:118-128`). A loss is a loss against the *part*, so it must reduce both measures by the same `taken` —
   which means `CapitalGainsAbsorption` must hold both maps and lower them in one place, not that a caller runs it twice.
6. **OQ-37's shift limb dissolves.** The shift is computed from the rest of total income (`atSlab`) and from the part's
   gain (`TaxByBasisNode.java:89-96`), and re-measuring the part with indexed cost does not change `atSlab`; so the
   shortfall is one rupee amount taken from both measures. The attribution limb stays open: a loss is applied to both,
   floored at zero, because neither Act says which measure it belongs to.
7. **`TaxByBasisNode:101` is the only shape that must change**: supply both bases and stop casting to `SlabLadder`. The
   slab path's cast (`:80-83`) is the *regime rate table*, which stays a ladder, so it is untouched.
8. **The acquisition-date boundary cannot be tested in TY 2025-26.** A long-term land sale by 31-3-2026 must have been
   acquired before 1-4-2024, i.e. always before 23-7-2024, so every TY 2025-26 land gain qualifies; only TY 2026-27 can
   hold a long-term sale acquired on or after 23-7-2024 (23-7-2024 → 1-9-2026 is 25 calendar months).
9. **Two measures on two records touch a wide construction surface** — 22 `new HeadsAfterSetOff(` and 4 `new
   CapitalGainsHead(` constructions in all, 21 and 2 of them in tests — so each record gains a convenience constructor that copies the gain into the
   indexed map, exactly as `CapContext` keeps its older one-base constructor (`CapContext.java:61-68`).
10. **The trace must name the winning limb, and today it does not.** The special-rate line is given
    `withLimitedBy(part.getKey().toString())` and the `CapResult.limitedBy()` is discarded (`TaxByBasisNode.java:100-104`),
    so a `LeastOf` would bind invisibly — against `LeastOf.Limb`'s own rule that "a winning limb with no name explains
    nothing" (`LeastOf.java:48-53`). No test asserts that line's text (checked: of the 125 lines in the tests that read `limitedBy()`, 45 are assertions and none is on a
    special-rate line).

**C1 — one item, or split again.** **(a) Recommended: one item.** The measure without the cap has no consumer (the exact

refused to write. (b) Split into "carry the indexed measure" and "the cap" — the first would ship a number nothing reads

**C3 — OQ-37, the shift and set-off.** **(a) Recommended: the same rupee amount on both measures, floored at zero.** The
shift is computed once from the rest of income (finding 6); a loss is against the part, so both measures pay it. (b)
Recompute the shift inside B — but the shift's input does not change, so this is the same answer arrived at twice; (c)
attribute losses to one measure by a declared order — a rule no Act states, and a question for the owner rather than a
default.

**C4 — where the qualification lives.** **(a) Recommended: a one-row rule in a new set** (`RS-CG-INDEXED-COST`), read by
the node per bucket, with every legal element — asset kind, the 23-7-2024 date, resident individual or HUF, the 1-4-2001
floor — in the row and its `IndexedCost` definition. (b) A Java predicate — refused: the standards name exactly this as
law-in-code.

**C5 — where the measure is computed.** **(a) Recommended: in `CapitalGainsNode`, through a package-private
`CapitalGainsIndexation`, beside `CapitalGainsCharges`.** The bucket's cost and dates exist only there, and the class

buckets are gone; (c) in a new pipeline node — a node with no input of its own, and a recomputation surface W-109 does not
need.

**C6 — the floor year.** **(a) Recommended: a definition type `IndexedCost(TaxYearId floorYear)` with a
`DefinitionJson` case**, so 1-4-2001 is pack data beside the row that uses it. (b) A Java constant — refused by the same
standard as C4.

**C7 — the trace.** **(a) Recommended: the special-rate line carries the cap's own `limitedBy` after the part's name**,
e.g. `LONG_TERM_OTHER; 12.5% of SPECIAL_RATE_GAINS`, and `; 200000 shifted to the basic exemption band` when the shift
took something. (b) Leave it as it is — then no golden can prove *which* limb bound, which is the only thing that
distinguishes the cap working from the cap being absent.

### Build (session 43) — what was built, and where it departed from this plan

Built as planned, with five departures, each forced by something the plan could not see:

1. **`RuleDefinition` had to be touched.** It is a sealed interface with an explicit `permits` clause
   (`rules/definition/RuleDefinition.java:11-18`), so `IndexedCost` did not compile until it was listed there. The
   plan's file list did not name this file.
2. **`DefinitionJson` hit the complexity limit.** The new `case "IndexedCost"` took `definition(...)` to a cyclomatic
   complexity of 13 against Checkstyle's 12, and `validate` stopped there — the build's own
   `CyclomaticComplexityCheck` message, not a reading. The two index
   definitions now share one branch and one helper, `indexDefinition(...)`, and `financialYear(...)` takes the field's
   location rather than appending `.financialYear` itself — the message for a bad `floorYear` reads `.floorYear`, as it
   should.
3. **The absorption floors the indexed measure.** The plan said "lowers both by the same `taken`" and did not say what
   happens when the loss takes more than the measure holds. Subtracting anyway would break `CapitalGainsHead`'s "nil or
   more" check; the measure is floored at zero instead, which is C3a's own "floored at zero" applied one step earlier.
4. **Violation 2 ran the other way.** Moving the qualifier's date to 2024-07-22 makes it *stricter*, so golden 5 stayed
   green; 2024-07-24 — one day past the boundary — is the direction that proves `ItemDateBefore` is exclusive, and it
   turns golden 5 red. Violations 2 and 3 both fail first on the measure assertion (`indexedPart` = 42,148.76 and
   7,86,324.79 where the test expects the gain) rather than on the charge the plan predicted; the charge fails next.
5. **A fifth list needed the two new ids** — `GeneratedConstantsTest.SHIPPED_RULE_IDS` (declared at `:36`) — beside
   the four the plan named. `CoverageCitationTest` also refused `RATE-CG-COST-INFLATION-INDEX` listed under 1961 s.112 /
   2025 s.197, since a listing must be named by the rule's own provisions; the rule is listed only where it names the
