## Plan for W-153b — the deductions' reach within the head by rule data

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** three deduction rows of the other-sources head say for
themselves which kinds of income they reach:
- the banker's commission row: interest on securities;
- the dividend-interest row: dividends and unit income, the year's limit;
- the other-expenditure row: the kinds that bear expenditure.

The kind tests at `OtherSourcesNode:169`, `:192`, `:210` and `DividendInterestAllowance:74` go (D-318, K2, K3).

**This plan raises C1 – C3.**

### Choices (recommended first)

- **C1 — how a row's reach is tested:**
  - **(a) recommended:** as W-153a does, the row's item conditions per item
    (`ctx.conditions().about(OtherSourcesNode.factsOf(item))`, `isAboutItem()` limbs only).
    - `DepositInterestDeduction`'s private `reaches` moves to `OtherSourcesNode.reaches(ctx, row, item)`, its three
      callers being other-sources consumers.
    - The sets stay `REGIME` / `EXACTLY_ONE`, so `ruleIdOf` and every nil line keep their row.
  - **(b)** turn the sets into `AT_MOST_ONE` on `REGIME_AND_CONDITIONS` and use `optionalRuleOf(set, facts)`. A row
    that does not reach an item is then absent, so every nil line must cite its `RuleIds` constant, and
    `DepositInterestDeduction` / `SurchargeNode` must change how they read the sets.
- **C2 — which paths read the reach:**
  - **(a) recommended:** the paths that hold a kind test or assume a reach:
    - `interest()`, `otherExpenditure()` (family pension, in-full kinds) and `interestIncluded` ask the commission
      row and the expenditure row;
    - `dividend()` and `DividendInterestAllowance.of` ask the dividend-interest row.

    `interest()` then calls `otherExpenditure()` after its income line. That gives the same lines in the same order
    (commission, then expenditure), and the duplicated block at `:168–179` goes.

    `dividend()`'s outright refusal of commission and other expenditure, and `compensationInterest()` /
    `wholeDeducted()`'s refusal of everything, stay as they are. They are each path's own law ("no deduction … other
    than interest expense", s.93(2); "no other deduction", s.93(1)(f); "the entire amount", s.93(1)(g), (h)). They
    belong to W-153c, which makes the path data.
  - **(b)** every path asks every row, so a dividend reached by the commission row would be deducted. That builds
    W-153c's path generality here.
- **C3 — the nil line's words where a row does not reach the item** (D-318 C3a):
  - **(a) recommended:**
    - commission: `"<where>: a banker's commission is not deducted against <KIND>"`, replacing "… against interest on
      securities only", which restates the list;
    - expenditure: `"<where>: expenditure is not deducted against <KIND>"`. That line is new and never written with
      the shipped data; it exists so a row edited to drop a kind leaves a nil line, not silence.

    The row on the line is unchanged.
  - **(b)** keep "against interest on securities only". It would be false the day the row reaches another kind.

### The build (C1a, C2a, C3a)

1. `OtherSourcesNode.reaches(NodeContext, RuleId, OtherSourceItem)`, moved from `DepositInterestDeduction`. That
   class calls it.
2. Pack, both versions each:

     `FORFEITED_ADVANCE`, `TERMINATION_COMPENSATION`, `OTHER`. Not the dividend kinds, interest on compensation, the
     whole-deducted kinds, or the four refused kinds (refused before any deduction; their law is deferred, D-202,
     D-193).
   - Each `_note` gains one sentence naming its condition. `SEC-OTHER-SOURCES-DEDUCTIONS` `_sources`
     disqualifiers[1] names the row's condition.
3. `OtherSourcesNode`:
   - `interest()` → income line, then `otherExpenditure()`, returning `interestIncluded`;
   - `otherExpenditure()`:

     - interest expense, where the dividend-interest row does not take it, plus other expenditure: deducted where
       the expenditure row reaches, else a nil line (C3a);
   - `interestIncluded`: the same two reach tests;
   - `dividend()`: interest expense joins the refused sum where the dividend-interest row does not reach the item.
4. `DividendInterestAllowance.of`: the base and claim are the items the row reaches.
5. Relock once: law 2025-26.88 → .89 and 2026-27.92 → .93, three rows each. Bump the pins in `RulePackLoadTest` and
   `TaxYear2025RulePackTest`.
