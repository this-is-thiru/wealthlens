**What this item is for (one sentence):** give the engine one way to handle a choice the assessee makes: list the
options, price each by recomputing the tax, pick one, and record every option and its price. Today each such choice
is either a fixed default (the set-off orders, OQ-28 / OQ-29) or entered as a plain fact (which houses are an own
residence).

**This plan proposes a split (§7) and raises choices C0–C6. Two of them (C1, C2) are law questions only you can
answer. Nothing is coded until you choose.**

### Findings

1. **The item is mis-sized.** By A1 and B2 it holds a sealed family with five variants, the resolver, the outcome,
   the regime-history log and its event. That is well over 1,000 lines. The pieces also depend on different things:
   - only two of the five choices have something to act on today: the set-off order (`SetOffNode`) and the regime
     (both pipelines exist);
   - the houses choice is W-083's own row (`HouseElectionNode`);
   - the history types need ids from W-096 and W-099.
2. **The history cannot be built here** without the B-003 trap. `ElectionEvent` holds a `DraftId` and
   `RegimeElectionHistory` a `ProfileId`, and neither exists. They are the persistence side: `taxplanning` keeps
   the log and projects it into the engine's `RegimeElectionSnapshot`. The engine needs only the snapshot, which is
   built. Their place is after W-099, before W-103 (`EngineInputsAssembler`, which does the projecting).
3. **Pricing must run whole pipelines until W-084.** `Recomputation` cannot serve a choice that bites early: the set
   -off order is read at `setoff`, before `gti`. So the resolver sits outside the graph, as `DR 15` C2's code has
   it, and runs `PipelineRunner.run` once per candidate. Candidates are few: at most six orders of three heads, two
   regimes.
4. **The two OQs are law questions, not pricing questions.** An election needs a statutory basis. If the assessee
   may not choose the order, there is nothing to optimise, and the declared default is the law's silence handled
   visibly, as now.
5. **OQ-29 cannot be priced in one year.** All brought-forward loss is set off against the same house-property
   income left, so this year's total set-off and tax are the same in every order. The order changes only later
   years: what expires after eight years, and what a later move to the new regime destroys (s.202(2)(b)(i), (3)).
   A one-year recomputation would price every order equal. If the assessee may choose, the choice can only be
   *stated*, never *optimised*, until a multi-year planner exists.
6. **The children choice has no case today** (counts against one per-child limit). Whether the gratuity aggregate
   holds a priced choice (several gratuities in one year against A − B) still has to be read in
   `NotifiedRetirementLimits`. That belongs to its own item (C0), not this plan.
