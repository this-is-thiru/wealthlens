## Plan for W-066e — retirement payments from two employers in one year

**What this item is for (one sentence):** stop the engine silently summing one retirement component paid by two
employers into a single payment measured on the first employer's facts. Refuse it loudly, proven by a test that fails
today, and place the real fix as its own row.

**This plan raises choices C0–C3. Nothing is coded until you choose.**

### What I read (session 21, 2026-10-18)

- **The row** (WI, after W-082c, D-223): *"first a test written to fail today and a loud refusal; then, in its own
  plan, each payment measured against its own employment and separation …, A − B shared between them, and the split
  priced by recomputation if the Act leaves it open"*.
- **The law, already read and quoted in the rule notes:**
  - `RET-GRATUITY-AGGREGATE`: S2 s.19(2)(a), *"if gratuity or gratuities was or were received from one or more than
    one employer in the same tax year …, the aggregate amount of deduction shall not exceed— A – B"*; 1961
    s.10(10)(iii) first and second provisos, to the same effect.
  - `RET-LEAVE-ENCASHMENT-AGGREGATE`: S2 s.19(2)(f), the same wording for leave salary; 1961 s.10(10AA)(ii) provisos.
  - `RET-GRATUITY-OTHER` and `RET-LEAVE-ENCASHMENT-OTHER` give each payment its own least-of: its own ten-month
    average and completed years, or its own leave at credit.
- **The code:**
  - `RetirementPayment` names a component and an entry, not an employment. `RetirementNode` refuses a component
    entered twice (l.111–119), so two employers' `GRATUITY` can only be entered as one payment.
  - `RetirementNode.paid` (l.208–213) sums the component across every employment.
  - `NotifiedRetirementLimits.employmentOf` takes the first employment paying it. `context` measures the ten-month
    average on that employment's terms, with the one payment's `Separation`.
  - `capped` applies A − B to that one line.
  - The other entries that read one employer's facts or a limit:
    - `OTHER_EMPLOYER_COMMUTATION` (one `commutedValueOfWholePension`);
    - `STATUTORY_GRATUITY` / `RETRENCHMENT` (one `StatutoryEntitlement` with one separation date);
    - `VOLUNTARY_RETIREMENT` (least of paid and ₹5,00,000).

    Each sums the component the same way.
  - The in-full entries are unaffected: `GOVERNMENT_GRATUITY`, `DEFENCE_RETIRING_GRATUITY`, `GOVERNMENT_COMMUTATION`,
    `FUND_COMMUTATION`, `APPROVED_SCHEME_RETRENCHMENT`, `GOVERNMENT_LEAVE_ENCASHMENT`. Summing them gives the same
    deduction as entering them apart.
  - `ComponentSet.Line` carries its `employment` index, so the number of employments paying a component is known.

### Findings

1. **The defect is wider than the row says.** Every entry measured on one employer's facts, or against a limit, is
   summed silently when two employers pay the component: six entries, not two.
2. **The real fix differs by entry.** For other gratuity and leave encashment the Acts' aggregate wording is read
   (above): each payment is measured on its own facts, and only the year's total is capped at A − B. Splitting that
   cap between the payments cannot change the tax, because both are in the same head and the deduction is their
   total. So `AggregateAllocationElection` still has nothing to price (D-223 holds). For commuted pension, voluntary
   retirement, statutory gratuity and retrenchment, how a second employer's payment is treated is **not** in the rule
   notes and would need reading.
3. **Fixing it needs an employment on each payment** (`RetirementPayment` gains which employment paid it, and one
   payment per component per employment). That touches about 50 test call sites and the node's lookups. Together
   with the aggregate cap over the year's payments, it is the second stage the row describes, too big to add to the
   refusal (§7).
