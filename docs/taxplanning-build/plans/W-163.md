## Plan for W-163 — statutory charges on each broker's card

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** the law's charges (securities transaction tax, stamp duty,
GST and the SEBI fee) are copied onto every broker's rate card, so this item decides with the owner whether they move
to one shared card per legal scope, or stay on the broker's card as the contract note's bill.

**Source:** audit T3 (`reviews/2026-10-21-law-in-code-audit.md:173`, D-315). Owner at placement: "Finding, decided at
plan". CLAUDE.md §4: "Every legal date, boundary, rate or qualifying list is tax-year-versioned rule data … so a law
that changes, lapses and returns is new data, not new code."

### What I read (session 37, 2026-10-28)

- **The 11 cards** in `backend/src/main/resources/data/charges/`. 10 of them carry statutory rules. The figures are
  identical across brokers for the same scope:

  | Scope (cards) | `STT` | `STAMP_DUTY` | `SEBI_FEE` | `GST` |
  |---|---|---|---|---|
  | equity delivery: Zerodha ×3, Upstox ×2, Fyers ×2 | 0.1 %, both sides | 0.015 %, buy | 0.0001 %, both | 18 % of `BROKERAGE, EXCHANGE_TXN, SEBI_FEE, IPFT, DP` |
  | equity intraday: Zerodha ×2 | 0.025 %, sell | 0.003 %, buy | 0.0001 %, both | 18 % of the same, without `DP` |
  | mutual fund: Zerodha ×1 | 0.001 %, sell | 0.005 %, buy | — | — |
  | maintenance: Zerodha ×1 | — | — | — | 18 % of `AMC` |

- **The window is wrong for the law.** Each statutory figure is windowed by its broker card's dates. Zerodha
  delivery's three cards break on 2026-03-01 and 2026-06-19 because of exchange and broker changes, not the law.
  Today a change in law would need a successor card for every broker and segment: 7 delivery, 2 intraday and 1 fund
  card.
- **The engine already merges two sources.** `ChargeEngine` (class Javadoc, lines 46 – 49) merges the broker's card
  and the instrument profile into one list ordered by `order`, so "a derived rule on the broker's card include[s] an
  instrument-sourced line in its base". A statutory card would be a third source of the same kind.
- **The resolver picks one card per trade.** `ChargeScheduleResolver` disqualifies a card that conflicts and ranks
  the rest by plan, exchange, segment and asset type. A statutory card cannot be a fourth candidate in that ranking,
  because it would compete with the broker's card instead of joining it.
- **One card is recorded per charge.** `UserChargeEntity` records one `scheduleId` / `scheduleCode` (`:109`, `:112`).
- **The sources for the rates.** STT's Sl. 1 – 3 rates (0.1 / 0.1 / 0.025 per cent) are in
  `sources/2026-10-20-securities-transaction-tax.md` row 1a (REPORTED, D-294), and Sl. 2A (0.001 per cent) is in row
  1d (READ). There is no source in the repo for stamp duty, the GST rate or the SEBI fee; the cards cite the brokers'
  pages (AC-2, verified 2026-09-08). Under either reading below that stays true unless sourced.
- **The tax side** holds STT's chargeability (`RS-STT-CHARGEABLE`), not its rate. `brokercharges` may not depend on
  `taxengine` (CLAUDE.md module table), so the tax engine cannot be the charge engine's rate source.

### Choices (recommended first)

- **C1 — the reading:**
  - **(a) recommended: a shared statutory card.** One card per legal scope, with no broker, windowed by the law's own
    dates. The engine merges it with the broker's card the way it merges the instrument profile today. A change in
    law then becomes one successor card, not ten. This is how the owner settled T4: the law lives in one place
    (D-375 – D-378).
  - **(b) accepted as the contract note's bill.** The card models what the broker bills, statutory lines included.
    The audit row is marked accepted with that reason, plus a test that fails when two brokers' cards valid on the
    same date and scope disagree on a statutory line. No engine change.
- **C2 — under (a), the codes that move:**
  - **(a) recommended:** the audit's four: `STT`, `STAMP_DUTY`, `SEBI_FEE` and `GST`, including the maintenance
    card's GST on `AMC`. GST's base names codes from the broker's card; the merged ordered list already allows that.
  - **(b)** those four plus `EXCHANGE_TXN` and `IPFT`. These are the exchange's levies, the same for every broker on
    an exchange, but not law.
- **C3 — under (a), the split:**
  - **(a) recommended:**
    - **W-163a:** the engine reads a statutory card. This covers the entity marker, its own resolution, the seeder,
      drift, validation, recording the statutory card's code beside the broker's, and tests.
    - **W-163b:** the statutory rules move from the 10 cards to the statutory cards with **the same figures**. Every
      existing simulate figure stays the same, proven by the existing engine tests and `staging-runbook.md` §4.
    - **W-163c:** each statutory rate is cited to the law. STT comes from the repo's sources; stamp duty, the GST
      rate and the SEBI fee need sourcing (the owner's ChatGPT-share route).
  - **(b)** W-163a and W-163b as one item. That is too large for one session (§7).

### What I am NOT doing

- Changing any figure. Under (a) the move keeps every rate; under (b) nothing moves.
- Writing anything before C1 is decided: each sub-item gets its own plan, with its own file list and probe.

### Post-deployment actions (R15)

- **Under (a):** nothing new. No deploy happens before Phases 0 – 14 (D-287). At that deploy, deploy runbook §1A
  clears every card and §1B seeds the new set, so no database ever holds the old cards beside the statutory ones. §1B's
  expected count changes from 11 cards to 11 broker cards plus the statutory cards; W-163b updates it.
- **Under (b):** none.

### Risks or ambiguities

1. **Under (a), a database seeded before W-163b would double-charge.** Its old broker cards still carry the
   statutory rules. The fresh start (§1A) is what prevents this. Drift (`GET /charge-schedules/drift`) would show it.
2. **The intraday GST base omits `DP`.** One statutory GST card can name `DP` for both scopes only if a missing line
   counts as 0 in a derived base. W-163a's plan checks `DerivedChargeCalculator` for this. Otherwise there is one GST
   row per scope.
3. **The fund card's STT 0.001 % on a sale.** Whether that is Sl. 2A (a sale on an exchange) or Sl. 5 (a sale to the
   Mutual Fund, which is a redemption) is not settled here. W-163c checks it.
