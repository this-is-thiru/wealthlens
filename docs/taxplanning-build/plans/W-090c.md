## Plan for W-090c — TY 2026-27's ITR forms: the split

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** to ship TY 2026-27's return forms for an individual and an
HUF as pack rows read from r.164, with the furnishing table and I19 and I20 on. This plan sizes the item and proposes a
split, because most of what r.164 asks cannot yet be written as a condition.

**Findings (each read this session):**
- F1: the law, read in full from the Gazette copy (Gazette 271092, pp.1662 – 1665: r.163(2) – r.166):
  - r.164(1): the rule governs a return "under section 263(1) or section 268(1)(a) or section 280, relating to the tax
    year commencing on the 1st April, 2026".
  - r.164(2): ITR-1 is for a resident individual other than RNOR whose total income includes income under:
    - (a) salaries or family pension (s.93(1)(d));
    - (b) house property, owning at most two, with no loss brought forward or to be carried forward under the head;
    - (c) other sources, except lottery winnings or race-horse income, with no loss under the head;
    - (d) capital gains, where there are "only long-term capital gains under section 198 not exceeding ₹ 125000" and no
      loss brought forward or to be carried forward under the head.
  - r.164(3)(a) – (n): the bars. Eleven are W-090a2's declarations. Two are computed: (e) a s.93 deduction other than
    93(1)(d), and (k) total income "exceeding fifty lakh rupees".
  - r.164(4): ITR-2 is for an individual "not being an individual to whom sub-rule (2) applies", or an HUF, without
    business or professional income.
  - r.164(5) – (6): ITR-4 is for a resident individual or HUF (other than RNOR) with business income "computed in
    accordance with special provisions referred to in section 58", and with only s.198 long-term gains up to
    ₹1,25,000. Bars (a) – (o) add (i) owning more than two house properties, (j) any loss brought forward or to be
    carried forward under any head, and (n) s.17(1)(d) income deferred under s.391(2) or s.392(3).
  - r.164(7): ITR-3 is for an individual or HUF not under (2), (4) or (5) "and having income under the head 'Profits or
    gains of business or profession'".
  - r.164(12) Table, the manners of furnishing:
    - Sl. 2: a person audited under s.63 uses a digital signature or EVC.
    - Sl. 3: any other person uses a digital signature, EVC, or electronic data followed by "Form ITR-V".
    - Sl. 4: an individual "of the age of eighty years or more at any time during the tax year" filing ITR-1 or ITR-4
      may also use "paper form".
- F2: return kinds. In the 2025 Act (Gazette 265620, pp.290 – 293, 301, 315):
  - s.263(4) is the belated return and s.263(5) the revised return. s.263(6) is the updated return, on ITR-UN by r.165.
  - s.268(1)(a) is a return on notice. s.280(1)(b) is a return in reassessment.
  - r.164(1) does not name s.263(4) or s.263(5). Whether the r.164 forms serve a belated or revised return is not in
    the text I read. See C3.
- F3: what a condition can read today (`rules/condition/`, `Condition.java:42` – `58`):
  - Residence: `ConditionContext` holds only `boolean resident` (`ConditionContext.java:39`), although the input knows
    RNOR (`Assessee.java:47` – `50`). "Other than not ordinarily resident" cannot be written.
  - Nothing reads the number of house properties, a loss brought forward or to be carried forward under a head, the
    kind of other-source income, the s.198 part of capital gains, or a s.93 deduction by clause. The only node a bar
    can read directly is `totalincome` (`NodeIds.java:47`).
  - `HasIncomeUnderHead(BUSINESS_OR_PROFESSION)` exists, but the engine computes no business income. Nothing tells
    business income computed under s.58 apart from business income computed otherwise, and nothing knows a s.63
    audit.
- F4: `WINNINGS` (lotteries, races, games, betting) is refused by the engine (`OtherSourceKind.java:94`), so (2)(c)'s
  lottery exception cannot be reached today. Race-horse income is not modelled.
- F5: r.164(6)(l) reads "deduction of tax under clause (d) of sub-section (1) of the said", which cannot be read as
  printed (D-520). It is ITR-4's bar only.
- F6: I20 ("no two forms simultaneously eligible") cannot be decided by evaluating conditions at pack load, because no
  person exists then. r.164 separates its forms in two ways:
  - by exclusion: ITR-2 excludes ITR-1, and ITR-3 excludes ITR-1, ITR-2 and ITR-4;
  - by opposite conditions: ITR-1 and ITR-2 have no business income, ITR-4 needs it.

**Choices for the owner:**
- **C1a (recommended): split W-090c into three, in this order.**
  - **W-090c1, the computed facts r.164's conditions read.** Each becomes a condition type over a fact the engine
    computes or holds, never a label such as "ITR-1 eligible":
    - the residential status (RNOR);
    - the number of house properties owned;
    - a loss brought forward, or to be carried forward, under a named head;
    - a loss under other sources;
    - capital gains other than s.198 long-term, and the s.198 long-term amount;
    - a s.93 deduction other than under 93(1)(d).

    I plan each against the node that computes it, and I will likely split this item again.
  - **W-090c2, the forms as data:**
    - TY 2026-27's ITR-1 and ITR-2 (C2a), the r.164(12) Table as each form's manners, the bars, and the cascade;
    - I19 on at pack load, I20 as the structural check of C4a, and I27 extended to `forms/` (W-090b's erratum).
  - **W-090c3, ITR-3 and ITR-4.** Parked with business income (C2a).
- C1b: build W-090c as one item. That means about six new condition types, their context, four forms and three
  checks in one change.
- **C2a (recommended): ITR-1 and ITR-2 now; ITR-3 and ITR-4 parked as W-090c3 until business income is computed.**
  - The engine refuses business income before any form is chosen (F3), so both forms could never be selected.
  - ITR-4 also needs facts nobody holds: s.58 presumptive computation and the s.63 audit for the Table's Sl. 2.
  - It also carries F5's unreadable clause.
  - I19 still holds: ITR-2 serves an individual and an HUF for each return kind.
  - C2b: write all four now, with s.58, the s.63 audit and race-horse income as new declarations. That ships two forms
    no one can reach.
- **C3a (recommended): ship the three return kinds r.164(1) names, `S263_1`, `S268_1_A` and `S280`.**
  - Record whether r.164's forms serve a belated (s.263(4)) or revised (s.263(5)) return as an open question, and
    W-091 refuses those kinds until it is answered from a Gazette source.
  - This matters, because the engine computes belated returns (`FilingFacts.furnishedOn` after the due date).
  - C3b: you rule now that s.263(4) and (5) returns use the same forms, as a recorded owner ruling.
  - C3c: leave belated and revised out with no open question.
- **C4a (recommended): I20 at pack load is structural.** Two forms that share a person type and a return kind must be
  separated in one of two ways:
  - one excludes the other;
  - one's positive conditions hold a condition and the other's its `Not`.

  Anything else is refused as possibly both eligible. This exactly fits r.164's construction (F6). C4b: I20 as a
  property test over generated persons in W-094 (the T21 tests) instead.
- **C5a (recommended): read r.164(2)'s "total income includes income under the head (a) – (d)" as a closed list.** ITR-1's
  income is under those heads only, each with its own limits, which is how (4) defines ITR-2 as everyone else without
  business income. C5b: read it as "at least one of", which leaves (4)'s exclusion meaningless for other income.
