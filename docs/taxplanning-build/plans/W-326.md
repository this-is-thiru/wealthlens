## Plan for W-326 — "what would change it", in rupees: design and split first

**What this item is for (one sentence, in my own words):** where a least-of limit binds, a line's explanation gains
A4.4's third sentence in rupees: how much more of the input that drives the binding limit would bring it to the next
limit, and what the figure would then be.

**Probe (no code run; a read of the cap shapes, the renderer and the rule data; 2026-11-14):**
- **Where it applies.** 28 rules have a `LeastOf` cap. Their limbs are `PercentOfBase` (38), `Fixed` (14),
  `AmountInExcess` (6), `PerUnitPerPeriod` (3) and `PercentOfBaseByLocation` (1).
  `LeastOf.apply` (`rules/cap/LeastOf.java`) evaluates every limb, but keeps only the winner's value and name.
- **The renderer has no context.** It cannot re-apply a shape, so the rupee figures must be computed where the cap is
  applied and carried on the line, as reasons are.
- **How each limb moves with its input** (read from each shape's `apply`):
  - `PercentOfBase`: at its rate on its base.
  - `AmountInExcess`: one rupee per rupee of its amount, above its threshold.
  - `PercentOfBaseByLocation`: at its band's rate.
  - `Fixed`: not at all.
  - `PerUnitPerPeriod`: with counts (months, children), not rupees.
  - `Rate.baseYielding` already turns a rupee gap back into the input that closes it.
- **Inputs.** The limbs read 26 distinct inputs (`BaseId`s). Many are not levers a person pulls:
  `AVERAGE_SALARY_BY_COMPLETED_YEARS`, `LOSS`, `AMOUNT_UNDER_STATUTE`, `INDEXED_SPECIAL_RATE_GAINS`, `TABLE_OFFICIAL_USE`
  and others. "Raise your loss by ₹X" is not advice.
- **Inputs in two limbs of one rule:** `TOTAL_INCOME` in `DED-RENT-PAID`, `EXPENDITURE` in both car perquisites, and
  `SALARY` in `SAL-HOUSE-RENT-ALLOWANCE`. Raising such an input moves two limbs at once.
- **A finding in the design doc:** A4.4's own example says rent could raise the HRA exemption "to the third limit of
  ₹6,60,000" against ₹6,00,000 of HRA received. HRA received is itself a limit, so the next limit there is
  ₹6,00,000. The example's figure is wrong; the shape of the sentence is right.

**The design (common to the choices):**
- `LeastOf` keeps every limb's value. Where the winning limb moves with exactly one input, that input is in no other
  limb, and the input has a word in the content, it publishes a **headroom fact**: the input, how much more of it, and
  the next limit's value. A `Fixed` or count-driven winner, a shared input, or a rule apportioned across two versions
  in the year publishes none.
- `CapResult` and `TraceLine` carry it, as they carry a reason. The renderer adds a third sentence from the content.
- **Which inputs are levers is content data:** an input gets the sentence only if the content words it (a new
  `terms.input`). Adding a lever is then a content change, not code.

**Choices for the owner:**
- **C1: the split.**
  - **C1a (recommended):** two items:
    - **W-326a, the engine:** limb values, the headroom fact on `CapResult` and `TraceLine`, and its test per shape.
      Nothing shows yet.
    - **W-326b, the words:** `terms.input` for the first levers, the sentence, the renderer's third sentence, and
      the compiler's checks.
  - C1b: one item.
- **C2: the first levers** (the inputs given words in W-326b). Recommended:
  - `RENT_PAID` (HRA, rent paid);
  - `HRA_RECEIVED`;
  - `ALLOWANCE_RECEIVED` (the allowances);
  - `AMOUNT_INCURRED` (leave travel and others);
  - `AMOUNT_CLAIMED`;
  - `TREATMENT_AND_STAY`;
  - `INTEREST`.
  Each is an amount the person pays, receives or claims. The rest stay silent until you add them.
- **C3: an input in two limbs** (`SALARY` for HRA, `EXPENDITURE` for the car perquisites).
  - **C3a (recommended):** no sentence. None is on C2's list today.
  - C3b: solve where the two moving limbs cross. That is more engine for inputs C2 leaves out.

**What I am NOT doing here:** any code. On approval, this places W-326a and W-326b on the board, and W-326 closes by
the split. Each sub-item has its own plan, probe, red test, review and commit.

**Post-deployment actions (R15):** none.

**Roles:**
- **Architect:** the figures are computed where the cap is applied, and carried as a fact. Which inputs count as levers
  is content data, not code.
- **PM:** this is A4.4's "advice" sentence: "paying ₹X more in rent would raise it to ₹Y". It turns a calculator into
  advice (`03-advisory.md:378 – 381`).
- **Senior engineer:** one method per limb shape, one new fact on two records, and one renderer sentence.
- **Automation tester:** a figure per shape worked by hand; the input-in-two-limbs and `Fixed` cases publish nothing.
- **Manual tester:** an HRA draft limited by rent shows "paying ₹… more in rent would raise it to ₹…".

**Risks:**
- The sentence is advice. It must be true for every rule that shows it, which is why it is limited to one moving limb
  and to inputs worded as levers.
- A4.4's example figure is wrong (above). I will not copy its numbers into a test.
